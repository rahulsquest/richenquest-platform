# File 67 — What company should RichenQuest actually be?

**Answered from comparators' real numbers, not from theory.**

---

## 1. The decisive arithmetic

> ### A pure consultancy cannot reach ₹100 crore. The math forecloses it.

At File 40's ₹1.2 lakh fee, ₹100 crore requires **~83,000 students**. IDP — a global listed company —
places a fraction of that annually.

**The comparator that settles it: Leverage Edu, FY26 revenue ₹375 crore**, up 112%, EBITDA-positive,
**175,000+ users**. That is roughly **₹21,000 of revenue per user** — nothing like a ₹1.2 lakh
consulting fee, because **consulting is not what they sell.**

They sell **admissions consultancy + education loans (Fly Finance, ₹280 crore facilitated in a single
month) + forex + accommodation.** The consultancy is the customer-acquisition channel. **The
financial services are the business.**

## 2. What every successful company in this sector has in common

| Company | What it actually monetises | Margin signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Flywire** | **Transaction fees + FX margin** on tuition payments | **$623M revenue, 67.3% adjusted gross margin, $37B processed** |
| **Leverage Edu** | Loans, forex, accommodation — consultancy acquires | ₹375 cr, EBITDA-positive |
| **LeapScholar** | **Counselling free**; monetises Leap Finance + test prep | — |
| MPOWER · Prodigy | Interest income, ~10%+ | recurring, capital-intensive |
| ApplyBoard · Adventus · MSM | University commission at marketplace scale | volume-dependent |
| Studyportals · Keystone | **Data and lead generation** — never touches a student | high margin, no service load |

> **Not one of them scaled on counselling fees. Every one of them monetises the money the student was
> going to move anyway.**

**The mistake almost every consultancy makes:** selling advice, which is labour, does not recur, does
not compound, and stops earning the day the student enrols.

## 3. The reframe

**A student going to Europe moves ₹20–40 lakh across a border over their degree** — tuition in
instalments, blocked account, living remittances, insurance, deposits.

**RichenQuest currently earns from one of those events. Flywire earns from all of them, at 67% gross
margin, and never advises anybody.**

| Business unit | Margin | Recurring | Automatable | Capital | Founder-dependent | Verdict |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|---|
| **Forex / remittance referral** | **very high** | ✅ **every payment** | ✅ | none | low | 🟢 **BUILD** |
| **Education loan referral** | high | partly | ✅ | none | low | 🟢 **BUILD** |

## 4. So what should RichenQuest be?

> ### Not a consultancy. A trusted-intelligence company that distributes student financial services — using Italy and DSU as the acquisition wedge.

**Three layers, in order:**

**1. The wedge — verified intelligence.** True Cost (File 61) and Italy/DSU (File 66). It costs
nothing to produce, it is structurally uncopyable by commission-funded competitors, and it is the
only asset RichenQuest owns outright today. **It acquires trust before it acquires a customer.**

**2. The relationship — admissions execution.** The fee funds the business early and it is how the
family's trust is earned. **But it is customer acquisition, not the destination.** File 57 already
found it cannot carry a premium price against free competitors.

**3. The business — the money the student moves anyway.** Forex, loans, insurance, blocked accounts.
**Near-zero marginal cost, recurring across the degree, and it keeps earning after graduation** when
the consultancy relationship has ended.

**Why this specific company and not Leverage Edu's:** Leverage Edu raised $40M+ and serves metros.
**RichenQuest has no capital and serves Bihar, Jharkhand, eastern UP and Nepal — a region the funded
players reach thinly.** Competing on capital is lost before it starts. **Competing on trust in a
region where trust is scarce and word of mouth is everything is winnable.**

## 5. The roadmap

| | Focus | Revenue engine | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yr 1** | **Italy/DSU specialist. First 100 students.** | Fee only | **₹1 cr** — 100 × ₹1.2 L |
| **Yr 2** | **Attach forex + loan referral to every student** | Fee + first ancillary | ₹3–5 cr |
| **Yr 3** | Insurance, blocked account, accommodation. **Ancillary exceeds fee revenue** | Fee + full ancillary stack | ₹10 cr |
| **Yr 5** | **License the intelligence B2B** to smaller consultancies who cannot build it | + recurring SaaS | ₹25–40 cr |
| **Yr 10** | **Own the financial rails for the corridor**, or be acquired by someone who wants them | transaction margin | **₹100 cr — only if Yr 3 ancillary works** |

**₹100 crore is conditional, and I will not pretend otherwise.** It requires the ancillary attach rate
to work by Year 3. **If it does not, this is a ₹20–30 crore company — which is an excellent outcome
and not the question asked.**

## 6. The sequencing trap

**File 44 §4 said: do not sign a partner before you have a student who needs them.** That still holds
— **approaching a forex or loan partner today, with zero students, spends the only conversation where
RichenQuest has leverage.**

> **So the financial-services business is the destination and cannot be started yet. The wedge can be
> started today, and the wedge is what makes the destination reachable.**

**The trigger to open financial-partner conversations is the first ten enrolled students**, because
ten students moving ₹2 crore between them is a conversation. Zero students is a request.

## 7. The one answer

**If I were the founder, I would build this:**

> **India's most trusted source of verified European study-cost and scholarship intelligence, serving
> low-income families in Bihar, Jharkhand, eastern UP and Nepal — monetised first through admissions
> fees, and ultimately through the financial services those families need to move ₹20–40 lakh across
> a border.**
>
> **Italy and DSU are the wedge, because they are where the demographic, the fee model, the
> competitive gap and the scholarship all point the same way — and where commission-funded
> competitors have no reason to follow.**

**Delete:** the ambition to be a large multi-country consultancy · work-visa B2B (unlicensed) ·
marketplace and SaaS platform ambitions (capital) · lending on balance sheet (capital and licence) ·
eight countries (File 65) · twelve of twenty universities.

**Why:** every one of them either needs capital RichenQuest does not have, a licence it cannot get, or
competes head-on with funded players in markets they already own. **The intelligence wedge needs none
of those, and it is the only thing in this analysis that a company with zero students and one founder
can start today.**

## 8. What changes tomorrow — almost nothing, and that is the test

**The Year 1 plan is unchanged: Italy research, first students, True Cost.** A strategy that survives
a first-principles rebuild without altering next week's work is a strategy worth trusting.

**What changes is the reason.** Those first 100 students are not the business — **they are the proof
that lets the financial-services business exist.** Which makes two things more important than they
looked:

1. **Every student must be a referral source.** The unit economics only work at low CAC.
2. **Trust is the actual product from day one**, because trust is what a family will later let you monetise. **A consultancy that misquotes a cost never gets to sell that family a loan.**
