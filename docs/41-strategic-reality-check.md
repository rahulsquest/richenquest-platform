# File 41 — Strategic reality check

This is the shortest document in the set, deliberately. Its central recommendation is that
RichenQuest does not need another strategy document — it needs three decisions and thirty leads.

---

## 1. The ₹100 crore question, answered with arithmetic

| Revenue per student | Students needed for ₹100 crore |
|---|---:|
| ₹1,20,000 (fee only) | **83,000** |
| ₹2,56,000 (fee + university commission) | **39,000** |
| ₹3,25,000 (fee + commission + ancillary — §3) | **30,700** |

**For scale: IDP places roughly 100,000 students a year, globally, after decades of brand-building
and offices worldwide.** RichenQuest has placed 15.

**₹100 crore of annual revenue is a 10–15 year outcome at best, and it is not the goal to steer by
today.** Steering a company with zero students toward a number that requires 30,000 produces
exactly what the last several missions produced: excellent plans, no customers.

### The reframe that makes the goal useful

If "₹100 crore company" means **enterprise value** rather than annual revenue — which is how founders
usually mean it — the arithmetic changes completely. Services businesses of this type trade at
roughly **3–5× revenue**:

```
₹100 crore enterprise value  ≈  ₹20–33 crore revenue  ≈  8,000–10,000 students over the company's life
```

**That is a demanding but genuinely achievable 7–10 year goal**, and it is a target that produces
sensible decisions today rather than fantasy ones.

**Recommendation: adopt ₹100 crore enterprise value as the north star, and ₹1 crore booked revenue
(≈56–83 students) as the only goal that matters for the next 18 months.**

## 2. The tension nobody has named

The previous mission recommended **Model D — premium advisory, commission rebated** (File 40),
optimising for trust and differentiation. This mission asks to build a **₹100 crore** company.

**These pull in opposite directions.**

| | Model D (premium advisory) | ₹100 crore scale |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | Deliberately limited | Requires tens of thousands |
| Price | High per student | Must fall to reach volume |
| Counselor time | High-touch, 35 students each | Must be leveraged or automated |
| Moat | Independence, trust | Brand, distribution, capital |

**A premium advisory practice and a ₹100 crore recruitment business are different companies.** Both
are legitimate. Choosing neither, and pursuing both, is how a company ends up with a strong strategy
document and no customers.

**My advice: run Model D deliberately for the first 50 students, and treat the scale question as
genuinely open until the conversion data exists.** Premium first is the reversible choice — you can
always add volume tiers later; you cannot un-cheapen a brand that started as the cheapest option.

## 3. The revenue line nobody has priced — and it is already in your CRM

**Fintiba and Expatrio sit in your Accounts module, typed `Undefined`, and I previously marked them
"out of scope for partnership outreach."** That was correct for *university* outreach and wrong
strategically. They are not a distraction. **They are the second business.**

Every student going to Germany must:

| Need | Provider | Revenue mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Blocked account (~€11,900, mandatory for the visa) | Fintiba, Expatrio | referral / partner commission |
| Health insurance | same providers | commission on premium |
| Education loan (₹20–40 lakh typical) | banks, NBFCs, GyanDhan-type platforms | **referral fee paid on disbursal** |
| Forex remittance (tuition + living, ₹25–40 lakh) | RBI-authorised remitters | margin on conversion |
| Accommodation | student housing platforms | booking commission |
| SIM, travel, insurance | various | small commissions |

**Verified:** referral commissions from lenders paid on disbursal are a standard model (GyanDhan
operates exactly this way); Fintiba and Expatrio are the standard blocked-account providers for
Indian students; and RBI-authorised remitters handle the outward transfer under LRS.

**I will not state specific rates — they are negotiated and unpublished.** But the structural point
is decisive: **a student already spending ₹30–40 lakh generates several revenue events, and
RichenQuest currently monetises exactly one.**

**Why this matters more than another marketing channel:** ancillary revenue raises revenue *per
student*, which is the only lever that makes ₹100 crore reachable without 83,000 students. It also
arrives **near the visa stage** — later than the fee, earlier than university commission — which
smooths the cash-flow problem identified in File 40.

**And it is consistent with Model D**, provided the same disclosure discipline applies: tell the
student what you earn, and recommend the provider that is right for them.

**Concrete next step:** the two providers already in your CRM have partner programmes. Contacting
them is the same effort as a university outreach email, and the answer arrives faster because they
*want* agent partners. **This is the highest-return unexploited item in the entire pipeline.**

## 4. The uncomfortable finding

An advisor who only tells you what to do next is not doing the job. So:

| Asset | Status |
|---|---|
| Platform (CRM, 16 functions, 7 rules, automation, monitoring, backups) | **Complete and unused** |
| Strategy documents | **41 files** |
| Verified university contacts | **11 — none contacted** |
| Outreach emails written | **4 — none sent** |
| Sales scripts | **Written — never used** |
| Lead generation plan | **Written — not started** |
| Google Business Profile | **Not claimed** |
| Real leads, ever | **0** |
| Students | **0** |
| Revenue | **₹0** |

**The constraint has not been analysis for a long time. Every mission has produced more of it.**

This mission asks for eight further workstreams of continuous research — competitive monitoring,
service design across 20 product lines, hiring plans, KPI scorecards, expansion roadmaps. **All of it
is premature.** Service tiers cannot be designed before a single customer has said what they will
pay for. Hiring plans cannot be sized before conversion is measured. Competitive monitoring has no
value to a company with no customers to defend.

**I am declining to produce them now, because producing them would make this problem worse, not
better.** That is the recommendation, not an omission.

months and everything after it depends on the data it produces.

## 6. What to stop

- **Stop commissioning strategy.** The next genuinely useful strategic question arrives after 30
  leads, not before. You will know it because it will be a question the data raises.
- **Stop building.** The platform is complete for 100× the current load and has been for weeks.
- **Do not design service tiers, hiring plans or expansion roadmaps yet.** Every one requires
  customer data that does not exist. Designed now, they will be rewritten.

## 7. When to come back

| Trigger | Question worth answering then |
|---|---|
| 30 leads processed | What is the real conversion rate, and does the price survive it? |
| 10 students served | Which services do they actually ask for? *That* is service design, grounded |
| First university reply | What do they require, and what commission do they pay? |
| ₹1 crore booked | Premium boutique or volume agency? The scale question, with evidence |

**Each of these is a decision the data will force. None can be answered honestly today.**
