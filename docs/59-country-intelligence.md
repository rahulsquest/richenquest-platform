> ### The ₹10–15 lakh Bachelor's does not exist in Europe.
>
> **The cheapest three-year Bachelor's anywhere above is ₹17.5 lakh** — and that is Czechia's
> cheapest programme in its cheapest city. **Living costs alone consume ₹10–22 lakh over three
> years.** This is not a gap in our research. It is arithmetic.

**We have been treating "Bachelor's ₹10–15 L" as an unfilled segment. It is an unfillable one**, on
family funds alone. The honest answers for that family are:

1. **A two-year Master's instead** — ₹11.7 L in Czechia, ₹13.9 L in Poland, ₹13.1 L in Italy. **This segment IS servable.**
2. **Italy with DSU** — the grant covers tuition, stipend, housing and meals, which removes most of the living cost that makes the arithmetic fail. **September only.**
3. **Part-time work**, where legal — **NOT VERIFIED per country, and must not be presented as a plan** until it is. A family that budgets on assumed student earnings is the classic way this goes wrong.

**Telling a ₹12 lakh Bachelor's family the truth early is worth more than a shortlist.** They can
switch to a Master's route, target Italy for September, or plan a loan — all of which beat discovering
it in month nine.

## 2. Germany, corrected

**The €11,904 blocked account is not a cost. It is the student's own living money**, released at
**€992/month** after arrival. Public tuition is effectively **€0** plus a semester contribution.

**So Germany is a liquidity barrier, not a price barrier** — File 42 framed it as "the wall" without
making that distinction, and the distinction changes who can go. A family that cannot *spend* ₹11 lakh
but can *borrow and park* it has a German route.

**But the total is still ₹22.6–23.6 L for a two-year Master's**, because the living money is real
money. **Germany is not cheap. It is tuition-free and expensive to live in.** Those are different
claims and we have been conflating them.

## 3. Hungary, challenged — and this one hurts

**Debrecen's tuition looks cheap: ₹10.5 L for a two-year Master's.** Add Hungarian living costs and
the country total is **₹21.9–33.6 L**.

⚠️ **I am not asserting Debrecen is unaffordable.** Hungarian living costs are the figure I refused to
record because sources disagree by a factor of two (File 54), and those figures are national averages
plus a visa-requirement number — **Debrecen is a small city and is very likely cheaper than Budapest.**

**But the direction is clear and uncomfortable: Poland's verified €4,300/yr is materially below
Hungary's likely €6,000+.** Our February flagship sits in one of the more expensive countries on this
list, and we have been calling it the budget option on tuition alone.

**This makes the Hungarian living-cost figure the second-most valuable unknown in the portfolio**,
after Vistula's tuition. It decides whether Debrecen is affordable for the segment we sell it to.

## 7. Risk

**The February 2027 campaign is built on Debrecen being the affordable option. At country level,
Hungary is one of the more expensive destinations researched.** If Hungarian living costs land at the
top of the disputed range, **Debrecen's Master's is a ₹25 L+ product, not a ₹15 L one** — and the
segment we have been aiming at cannot afford it.

**That does not stop the campaign.** Debrecen's access story — IELTS 5.5, interview route, 4-year gap
— is real and unmatched. **But it must be sold on access, to families who can fund it, rather than on
price to families who cannot.**
