## 1. Solved myself: living costs (category 1)

I have flagged this as "the next task" three times without doing it. Done now, and the two countries
came out differently — which is the point of doing the work rather than estimating.

### Poland — derivable from statute, so it is now recorded

Polish residence-permit law sets the test as a **formula, not a flat number**: monthly funds after
accommodation costs must exceed **PLN 776** for a single person, **plus** actual accommodation, **plus
PLN 200** for return travel.

Worked from the official example — a student in a dormitory at PLN 750/month must show **PLN 1,526/month**:

> **≈ PLN 1,526/month ≈ €355/month ≈ €4,300/year, accommodation included.**

**Recorded on Vistula's record as €4,300/yr**, with the basis stated. It is consistent with Vistula's
own published dormitory cost of €210/month, which is a useful independent check.

**This is the first living-cost figure in the portfolio with a defensible basis**, and it makes
Vistula's budget comparison a real one rather than tuition-only.

### Hungary — sources conflict by a factor of two, so the field stays empty

| Source | Required funds |
|---|---|
| Commonly cited | **HUF 200,000/month** (≈ €500) |
| Also cited | **HUF 98,000/month** |
| General Hungarian living cost | €640/month (**national average, not Debrecen** — a smaller city) |

**A factor-of-two disagreement on a visa requirement is not a rounding difference.** Quoting the low
figure to a family could cost them the visa; quoting the high one could cost them the decision.

> **So Debrecen's `Living_Cost_EUR_Year` remains empty.** It keeps Debrecen from reaching 100%
> research completeness, and that is correct — *Unknown is acceptable, incorrect is not.*

**Founder-facing consequence:** confirm the current subsistence figure with the Hungarian consulate
handling the application, per intake. It goes on the SOP-0 list.

## 2. Solved myself: what is a student actually worth (category 1)

**This is the analysis F1 has been missing.** A founder does not leave a pricing decision open for ten
phases out of indecision — they leave it open because the inputs are absent.

| Revenue line | Per enrolled student | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Consulting fee** | **the decision itself** | 🔴 **F1 — unset.** File 40 recommends ₹1,20,000 for the first ten |
| Education loan referral | **₹3,000**, plus ₹10,000 per 5 referrals | ✅ **VERIFIED** (GyanDhan) |
| University commission | **UNKNOWN for all 20** | 🔴 P14-1. Germany-private rates were verified at ~12% of tuition ≈ ₹1,36,000, but **no university has confirmed anything to RichenQuest** |
| Blocked account (Fintiba / Expatrio) | rate **unpublished** | ❌ unknown |
| Insurance · forex · accommodation · SIM · travel · banking | **all unpublished** | ❌ unknown |

### The finding

> **Exactly one ancillary revenue line in the entire business is verified, and it is worth ₹3,000.**

Every other ancillary stream — the ones Revenue Engine 3 asks me to build out — is **commercially
unquantifiable today.** Not small: *unknown*. And an unknown cannot be planned against.

**Therefore, and this is the argument for F1:**

> **The consulting fee is not one revenue line among many. Right now it is essentially the entire
> business.** Ancillary revenue is ₹3,000 verified, and commission is a question mark that only
> P14-1 can answer.

**Pricing at the bottom of File 40's band on the assumption that "commission and ancillaries will
make up the difference" would be pricing against numbers that do not exist.** If the fee is set low
and commission turns out to be zero on public universities — which File 42 predicts for exactly the
affordable institutions this portfolio is built on — there is no second line to fall back on.

**I am not setting the price. But the missing input is now supplied: assume ancillary ≈ ₹3,000 and
commission ≈ ₹0 until a university says otherwise, and set the fee so the business works on that
basis.**

### Why I am not building out Revenue Engine 3 further

Approaching Fintiba, forex or accommodation partners today means asking for terms with **zero
students and zero volume** — the one conversation where RichenQuest has no leverage, spent at the
only moment it cannot be repeated. File 44 §4 already sequenced these as **Wave 2, after the first
student signs.** That sequencing was right and this pass does not change it.

## 3. Solved myself: the referral engine (category 1)

**Revenue Engine 4, and it is the highest-ROI lead source available**, for a reason that is specific
to RichenQuest:

> **`claims.json` records 1,000+ students guided, and the CRM holds zero leads.** The largest
> untapped asset in the business is a past-student base that has never been asked for anything.

**F6 ("message 50 past students") has been open for many phases** — because 50 individually written
messages is two hours of work nobody starts. **So I removed the reason it stalls**, the same way the
Wave 1 emails went from 30 minutes to 5.

`outreach/REFERRAL-ENGINE.md` contains ready-to-send WhatsApp text for the four audiences that can
realistically produce February/March 2027 enquiries, plus the programme structure.

**Design decisions worth stating:**

| Decision | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Referrers are asked for an introduction, not a sale** | A past student who feels turned into a salesperson stops replying. An introduction costs them nothing and preserves the relationship |
| **No cash incentive named until F1 is set** | A referral fee is a fraction of a price that does not exist. Naming a number now means changing it later, which is worse than waiting |
| **Coaching institutes and school teachers are the highest-yield channel** | They meet the target demographic in cohorts, at exactly the age of the decision, and in Bihar/Jharkhand they are trusted more than any brand |
| **Nothing is automated** | Referral messages from a real person convert; referral messages from a system are spam, and one blast can burn a channel permanently |
