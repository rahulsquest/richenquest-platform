# The RichenQuest Constitution

**Version 1.0 — adopted 2026-07-25 · Ratified by Rahul Kumar, Founder**

The operating system of RichenQuest. Supersedes nothing; `docs/19-master-constitution.md`
is retained as the engineering-governance annex referenced in Article 11.

---

## PREAMBLE — how to use this document

This document exists to be **cited, not admired**. Every article is numbered so that anyone
can say "Article 7.4" in a meeting and settle a question with a shared reference.

**A principle that has never cost us anything is not yet a principle.** Every article below
should, at some point, make us slower, smaller, or poorer than we could have been. If one
never does, it is decoration — delete it.

**But a principle that cannot survive reality is also not a principle.** This document is
written to be kept for twenty-five years by a company that must also earn money, hire people,
raise capital, and grow. Where idealism and durability conflict, we choose durability —
because a principle abandoned under pressure is worse than one never adopted.

**Definition — "the individual".** Throughout this document, *the individual* means the person
whose future is being decided. Today that is almost always a student. In time it will also mean
professionals, researchers, clinicians, founders, and their families. The articles are written
so that this expansion requires no rewrite.

**If everyone here vanished tomorrow**, this document, the claims library, and the public
disclosure register are enough to rebuild the company.

## ARTICLE 0 — PRECEDENCE AND THE DECISION RULE

### 0.1 Order of precedence

When two articles conflict, the lower number wins.

1. **Safety, wellbeing and law.** Never traded for anything below.
2. **Independence of recommendation** (Article 5).
3. **Truthfulness** — we do not state what we cannot evidence.
4. **The individual's interest** — above ours, above a partner's, above a founder's preference.
5. **Long-term trust.**
6. **Durability of the company** — we must survive to keep any of the above.
7. **Growth.**

Note that durability outranks growth and sits below trust. A company that dies is of no use to
anyone; a company that survives by spending its trust has already failed at something harder
to rebuild than revenue.

### 0.2 The decision rule — the Disclosure–Durability Test

When the answer is not obvious, apply both halves. A decision must pass **both**.

> **Disclosure:** Would we be willing to explain this decision, in full and unprompted, to
> the individual it affects — including how we are paid?
>
> **Durability:** Can the company afford to make this same decision a thousand more times?

Failing disclosure means the decision is not honest.
Failing durability means it is not a policy, it is a gesture.

If a decision passes disclosure but fails durability, **redesign the model, not the ethics.**
That is the discipline this company is built on.

only master is the person receiving it.

otherwise reach, it is not mission work. It may still be necessary. Do not confuse the two.

## ARTICLE 4 — CORE VALUES

Six. Each is a behaviour, not a feeling.

**4.1 Independence before growth.** We would rather grow more slowly than have our advice
become something a reasonable person would discount.

**4.2 Truth in the room where it costs.** Honesty in a comfortable room is manners. Honesty
when it loses the engagement is character.

**4.3 Evidence over assertion.** "I believe" and "we can show" are different sentences. Only
one belongs on a public page.

**4.4 The person, not the transaction.** We are accountable for where someone is in five years,
not for what they signed this month.

**4.5 Proof compounds; noise decays.** We will lose short races to those who overstate. That is
an acceptable price, and a temporary one.

**4.6 Custodianship.** We hold people's documents, money, and hopes. None of it becomes ours.

## ARTICLE 5 — INDEPENDENCE AND CONFLICT GOVERNANCE

*This is the heart of the Constitution. It replaces any rule that would have frozen our business
model. RichenQuest may earn revenue from individuals, universities, employers, institutions,
governments and research partners. What is governed here is not the **source** of revenue but the
**independence of the recommendation**.*

*We do not consider commercial relationships with universities to be a compromise. Universities
are partners in the same outcome. Conflicts of interest are a normal feature of any advisory
business; the difference between a trusted institution and an untrusted one is whether those
conflicts are **structurally managed and openly disclosed** — or left implicit.*

### 5.1 The Independence Principle

**No recommendation made to an individual may be influenced, in fact or in appearance, by any
commercial relationship RichenQuest holds.**

Operationally, a recommendation is independent when it can be **reproduced from documented
criteria that make no reference to any commercial term.** If the reasoning cannot be
reconstructed without knowing who pays us, it was not independent.

### 5.2 Separation of duties

- The people who negotiate commercial relationships do not make recommendations to individuals.
- The people who advise individuals are not measured, paid, or promoted on which institution an
  individual chooses.
- Neither function reports to the other on outcomes.

This separation is structural, not cultural. It must survive a change of management.

### 5.3 The compensation firewall

**No individual's pay, bonus, quota, or performance review at RichenQuest may vary according to
which institution, country, employer, or programme a person selects.**

The company may hold differential commercial arrangements. **The adviser may never feel them.**
This is the mechanism that makes 5.1 real rather than aspirational, and it is what allows the
company's business model to evolve without eroding trust.

### 5.4 Disclosure

- Every commercial relationship is recorded in a **public disclosure register**: the counterparty,
  the nature of the arrangement, and its commercial basis.
- Where a recommendation involves a party we have a relationship with, that relationship is
  disclosed **at the point of recommendation** — in plain language, in the same view, not in a
  policy page elsewhere.
- Disclosure is written for the reader, not for the regulator.

### 5.5 Audit for steering

- Recommendation patterns are audited at least annually against the disclosure register.
- If individuals are directed toward commercially-related parties at a materially higher rate
  than the documented criteria predict, that finding is investigated and **published**, together
  with the corrective action.
- The audit method is public. Where practical, the audit is independent.

### 5.6 Refusal of influence

Any request from any party for preferential placement, quotas, steering, or "focus" is declined
and recorded in the register. **We decline the request, not the relationship** — a partner may
ask once without becoming an adversary.

### 5.7 The escape hatch that is not one

If the company ever concludes that these governance mechanisms are commercially unsustainable,
the honest course is to **change them openly and explain why** (Article 33.2). Quietly weakening
them while continuing to claim independence is the one failure this document does not survive.

## ARTICLE 6 — NON-NEGOTIABLES

These may not be waived by management, a board, or an investor. They may be amended only under
Article 33.2, in public.

**6.1** Recommendations are independent, and conflicts are disclosed (Article 5).
**6.2** No individual's compensation varies by the destination chosen (Article 5.3).
**6.3** We state publicly only what we can produce records for.
**6.4** We decline engagements we do not believe we can serve — including when we need the revenue.
**6.5** The individual owns their record. It leaves with them, complete and portable, on request.
**6.6** We do not sell personal data or attention.
**6.7** A named human is accountable for every recommendation affecting a person's future.
**6.8** We present cost, likelihood and risk before benefits.
**6.9** No one is penalised for losing revenue by telling the truth.
**6.10** We do not manufacture urgency, scarcity, or confidence.

**If instructed to breach any of these, you are required to refuse and required to escalate
(Article 29.3). The instruction is void regardless of its source, including the founder.**

## ARTICLE 7 — DECISION FRAMEWORK

**7.1 The Five Questions.** In order. Stop at the first failure.

1. **Whose interest does this serve first?** If ours or a partner's above the individual's, stop.
2. **Would I reach the same conclusion if we earned nothing from any party involved?**
3. **Can we evidence it?**
4. **Would I be comfortable if the individual read this decision in full, including the reasoning
   and the commercials?**
5. **Does it hold in ten years, or only this quarter?**

**7.2 Door test.** Reversible decisions: decide quickly, alone, correct later. Irreversible ones —
pricing model, partnership terms, data handling, anything touching Articles 5 or 6 — are slow,
written, and escalated.

**7.3 Speed.** Most decisions are reversible. Bias to action; the cost of a slow company is paid
by people waiting for answers about their lives.

**7.4 Disagreement.** Argue hard and in writing before the decision. Once made, commit publicly
and completely, including the parts you lost. Re-litigating a settled decision privately is a
failure of integrity, not of strategy.

**7.5 When you do not know.** Say "I don't know — I will find out by [time]." Never close a gap
with confidence. Guessing in front of a family making an irreversible decision is the most
damaging thing anyone here can do.

**7.6 Precedent.** A hard call, once made, is written down with its reasoning so the next person
does not start from zero. Decisions are institutional property.

## ARTICLE 8 — LEADERSHIP PRINCIPLES

**8.1 Carry the cost visibly.** A leader's authority here comes partly from having personally
declined revenue on principle, and said so.

**8.2 Absorb pressure; do not transmit it.** Commercial anxiety stops with you. The moment an
adviser feels it, judgement starts to bend.

**8.3 Celebrate the correct refusal** publicly and by name — at least as loudly as a win.

**8.4 Be replaceable.** Anything only you can do is an outage waiting to happen.

**8.5 Go and see.** Sit in on real advisory conversations monthly; read real files. Strategy built
only on dashboards drifts from reality.

**8.6 Give the number first.** Never soften poor performance with narrative. Figure, then plan.

**8.7 Decide, or name who decides.** Ambiguous ownership is a leadership failure.

**8.8 Protect the dissenter.** The person who says "I think this breaches Article 5" is the most
valuable person in the room, including when they are wrong.

---

## ARTICLE 9 — HIRING PRINCIPLES

**9.1 The cost-of-truth question.** Ask every candidate: *"Tell me about a time telling the truth
cost you something."* A candidate with no credible answer is not hired into an advisory role,
however strong their other skills.

**9.2 Hire for the hard conversation.** Can this person tell a hopeful family, kindly and clearly,
that they should wait a year? If not, they cannot advise here.

**9.3 Watch what they optimise.** A candidate whose first question is the per-placement incentive
has answered a different question than the one asked.

**9.4 Character is not trainable; craft is.** Hire judgement and honesty; teach admissions, visas,
compliance and systems.

**9.5 Raise the bar.** Every hire should be better than the median person already doing that work.
When unsure, do not hire.

**9.6 Local understanding is expertise.** Someone who genuinely understands what a family in a
tier-2 city fears is more valuable than a polished CV without it. Pay accordingly.

**9.7 Never hire to rescue a quarter.** Understaffed and sound beats staffed and compromised.

## ARTICLE 10 — PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

**10.1 Shipped beats announced.** Nothing is presented as existing before it exists. Roadmaps are
labelled as roadmaps.

**10.2 A free tool must be genuinely useful on its own.** If its only value is capturing a contact,
it is bait — remove it.

**10.3 No contact wall in front of value.** A person may reach a real answer without surrendering
their phone number.

**10.4 The record is theirs.** Export is one action, complete, and always available. Designing for
lock-in is prohibited.

**10.5 Show the reasoning.** Anything that ranks, scores, or recommends must show why — including
the arguments against.

**10.6 Design for the exit.** Leaving must be as easy as joining. Retention through friction is
prohibited.

**10.7 Boring and reliable beats clever and fragile.** People are managing visas, deadlines and
life savings.

**10.8 Build for the next user, not only today's.** Structures that assume "student" where they
could assume "individual" create rewrites later (see Preamble).

## ARTICLE 11 — TECHNOLOGY PRINCIPLES

**11.1 Build for their device, not ours.** The reference user is a mid-range Android phone on a
congested mobile network. What fails there does not ship.

**11.2 Automate our own discipline.** Where a principle can be enforced by a machine, enforce it
by a machine. Our build already fails when public claims exceed the evidence library; extend that
pattern. Discipline that depends on memory eventually fails.

**11.3 Prefer boring technology.** Fewer dependencies, fewer moving parts, longer support life.

**11.4 Own the record; rent everything else.** The integrity and portability of the individual's
record is never dependent on a vendor who could withhold it.

**11.5 Observable or it does not exist.** Every automated process reports whether it ran. A system
that stops working silently is worse than one that never worked.

**11.6 No infrastructure we would not describe in plain words** on the same page as the person
using it.

**11.7 Engineering governance.** Detailed standards live in the engineering annex
(`docs/19-master-constitution.md`) and the ADR record. They are subordinate to this document.

## ARTICLE 12 — AI PRINCIPLES

We operate under the EU AI Act (education AI is high-risk) and India's DPDP Act. These are the
floor, not the ambition.

**12.1 AI advises; people decide.** No automated system may determine an outcome affecting a
person's future. A named human signs every recommendation.

**12.2 We do not screen people on behalf of institutions.** We will not build or operate systems
that rank, filter, or reject applicants for a third party. That is the point at which advisory
independence would end.

**12.3 No profiling of minors.** No behavioural tracking or profiling of under-18s. Where we serve
schools, we act on their instruction as a processor.

**12.4 Accurate naming.** A rules engine is called a rules engine. Inflating the word "AI" is the
same category of error as inflating an outcome statistic.

**12.5 Explainable or unshipped.** If we cannot explain an output to the individual in one
paragraph, it does not go live.

**12.6 Audit for algorithmic steering.** Recommendation systems are tested for systematic bias
toward particular destinations or partners. Evidence from school-counselling software elsewhere
shows algorithmic steering causes measurable harm. Test regularly; publish the method (Article 5.5).

**12.7 No inference we could not ask for directly.** AI may not become the reason we know something
about a person that we would not have been comfortable asking them.

## ARTICLE 13 — PRINCIPLES FOR THE PEOPLE WE SERVE

**13.1 The individual is the client.** Where an individual and a family member disagree, the
individual's interest governs; the family's understanding must still be earned.

**13.2 Hardest question first.** Total cost, realistic likelihood, and the downside — before any
benefit is described.

**13.3 Nobody leaves confused.** Every substantive conversation ends with a written summary in
plain language, which they keep regardless of whether they proceed.

**13.4 Say the number.** Never "affordable". Always the figure, and what it excludes.

**13.5 "Not yet" is a legitimate recommendation** and must be given when true — together with what
would change it.

**13.6 First disclosure.** If a fact would change their decision, they hear it from us before they
discover it elsewhere.

**13.7 Accountability continues after arrival.** Someone who has landed and is struggling is still
ours.

## ARTICLE 14 — PARTNERSHIP PRINCIPLES

*Universities, employers, governments, schools and research institutions are partners in the same
outcome. This article enables those relationships and governs them.*

**14.1 Partnerships are welcome and are governed, not avoided.** Every relationship operates under
Article 5.

**14.2 Terms are documented and disclosed** in the public register (Article 5.4).

**14.3 No arrangement may narrow the options presented to an individual.** Exclusivity that
restricts what a person may consider is not accepted.

**14.4 Steering requests are declined and recorded** (Article 5.6).

**14.5 We represent the individual to the institution.** Where we also provide services to the
institution, that work is delivered by a separated function (Article 5.2), and both sides are told.

**14.6 Announced means signed.** No relationship appears on any public surface before it is
executed.

**14.7 Partners are held to our data and safeguarding standards,** or the relationship does not
proceed.

**14.8 We compete by building something better** — never by disparaging a university, a competitor,
or an individual. Where we criticise, we criticise **structures and incentives**, not people or
organisations.

## ARTICLE 15 — MARKETING PRINCIPLES

**15.1 Never claim what the product cannot do today.**
**15.2 No manufactured urgency** — no countdowns, no artificial scarcity. Real deadlines, stated
once, accurately.
**15.3 Publish our limits** — what we do not know, have not measured, and cannot promise.
**15.4 No testimonial without written consent; no logo without a signature; no statistic without a
record.**
**15.5 Never buy an outcome we should earn** — no paid reviews, no incentivised ratings.
**15.6 Comparison is structural and respectful.** We describe how incentive structures differ. We
do not characterise other organisations or their people as dishonest.
**15.7 Confidence, not superiority.** We are building an alternative, not issuing a verdict on
everyone else.

## ARTICLE 16 — COMMUNICATION PRINCIPLES

**16.1 Bad news travels first and fastest.** The delay is the offence, not the problem.
**16.2 Write it down.** A decision existing only in someone's head is not a decision.
**16.3 Plain language always.** If a reader without specialist knowledge cannot follow it, rewrite
it. Jargon is usually concealment.
**16.4 Brevity is respect.**
**16.5 Own the error in the first sentence.** No passive voice, no "issues were experienced".
**16.6 Silence is a decision.** Not replying to a worried family is an act with consequences.

---

## ARTICLE 17 — DESIGN PRINCIPLES

**17.1 Clarity over impression.** If a visual makes us look better without making the reader
understand more, remove it.
**17.2 Never project confidence the evidence does not support.** Uncertainty is shown as
uncertainty.
**17.3 No dark patterns.** No pre-ticked consent, no hidden costs, no buried unsubscribe.
**17.4 The constrained device is the design target.**
**17.5 Space is a feature.** Crowding is a symptom of not having decided what matters.
**17.6 Accessible by default** — contrast, tap targets, keyboard, screen readers.
**17.7 Motion must carry meaning.** Decoration that costs load time is a tax paid by the least
well-connected user.

## ARTICLE 18 — DATA PRINCIPLES

**18.1 Collect the minimum.** If we cannot name the decision a field informs, we do not collect it.
**18.2 The individual owns it.** Portable export, always available.
**18.3 Deletion is honoured completely,** including backups, within the stated window.
**18.4 Never sold, never brokered, never used for advertising targeting.**
**18.5 Sensitive records** — identity documents, financials, academic records, health information —
are need-to-know, accessed by named individuals, with an audit trail.
**18.6 DPDP and GDPR are the floor.** Where they differ, apply the stricter.
**18.7 Breaches are disclosed to affected people first,** quickly and in plain language — before
regulators, press, or polish.

## ARTICLE 19 — FINANCIAL PRINCIPLES

**19.1 Solvency is a principle, not a constraint on principles.** A company that cannot pay its
people cannot keep its promises. Durability outranks growth (Article 0.1).

**19.2 Diversify revenue so no single counterparty can dictate our advice.** Concentration is a
governance risk before it is a financial one. Where any single counterparty exceeds a material
share of revenue, that fact is reported to leadership and reviewed.

**19.3 Price is published** for services offered to individuals, and stated in full, including what
it excludes.

**19.4 Never price on desperation.** Fees do not rise because a deadline is close or an applicant
is anxious.

**19.5 Take capital from investors who have read this document.** Alignment is a term of investment.
Where an investor's expectations conflict with Articles 5 or 6, we decline the capital.

**19.6 Spend as if the money must last.** Growth funded by unsustainable unit economics converts
future trust into present revenue.

**19.7 Refunds and exits are honoured in the spirit of the agreement,** not the narrowest reading
of it.

**19.8 Financial reality is shared with the team honestly** — runway and performance are not
mysteries kept from the people whose judgement depends on knowing them.

## ARTICLE 20 — INNOVATION PRINCIPLES

**20.1 Innovate on the constraint, not the claim.** Our hard problems are trust, verification,
access, and cost. Novelty that does not address one of these is decoration.

**20.2 Small, reversible experiments first.** Prefer the version that can be withdrawn without
harming anyone.

**20.3 Never experiment on someone's irreversible decision.** A/B tests may not run on advice,
eligibility, or anything affecting a person's outcome. Test the interface; never the counsel.

**20.4 A failed experiment that produced knowledge is a success** and is written up as such.

**20.5 Ship narrow and real over broad and notional.** One working capability beats ten announced.

**20.6 Adopt new technology when it serves the individual,** not when it serves our positioning.

## ARTICLE 21 — CONTINUOUS LEARNING

**21.1 The world changes faster than our documents.** Visa rules, intakes, funding and regulation
shift constantly. Currency of knowledge is a job requirement, not a virtue.

**21.2 Every published fact has an owner and a review date.** Unreviewed facts are removed, not
left to rot.

**21.3 Post-mortems on real outcomes.** When someone we advised had a poor outcome, we examine why,
without blame, and change something.

**21.4 Teach what you learn.** Knowledge held by one person is a liability.

**21.5 Learn from those outside our field.** The problems we face — trust in advice, verification,
conflicted incentives — were solved partially elsewhere. Study medicine, audit, and fiduciary
finance before inventing.

**21.6 Update publicly when we were wrong.** Corrections are dated and kept, not silently edited.

## ARTICLE 22 — COMMUNITY AND ALUMNI

**22.1 The relationship does not end at arrival.** A person we advised is a member of this
community for life, whether or not they ever pay us again.

**22.2 Alumni are never a lead source first.** They are people we owe something to. Any commercial
use of the community requires their explicit, revocable consent.

**22.3 Mentorship flows downward by design.** People who have made the journey are the best guides
for those beginning it. Build the mechanisms; never coerce participation.

**22.4 The network's value belongs to its members,** not only to the company.

**22.5 Safeguarding is mandatory** in any mentor relationship, especially where minors are involved:
verification, boundaries, reporting routes, and human oversight.

**22.6 Publish outcomes over time,** including disappointing ones. A community built only on
successes is a marketing asset, not a community.

## ARTICLE 23 — OPEN STANDARDS AND INTEROPERABILITY

**23.1 Build on open standards wherever they exist** — verifiable credentials, recognised
qualification frameworks, and portable identity systems — rather than proprietary formats.

**23.2 The record must be readable without us.** Export in documented, non-proprietary formats.

**23.3 Interoperate rather than capture.** Where another organisation holds a record a person owns,
we help them move it, including away from us.

**23.4 Do not build a moat out of a person's own data.** Our advantage must come from judgement,
service and trust — never from making departure expensive.

**23.5 Contribute back.** Where we develop something the wider field needs — a verification method,
a disclosure format, an audit technique — we publish it.

**23.6 Depend on no single national system as a sole foundation.** Identity and credential
infrastructures change with politics; design for substitution.

## ARTICLE 24 — HUMILITY AND EVIDENCE-BASED DECISION MAKING

**24.1 Strong opinions, held to evidence.** Confidence must be proportional to the evidence behind
it, and must fall when the evidence does.

**24.2 Say what would change your mind** before the debate begins. A position that no evidence
could alter is a belief, not an analysis.

**24.3 Distinguish what we know, what we infer, and what we assume** — explicitly, in writing.

**24.4 Sample size discipline.** Our own experience is a small sample. Treat it accordingly,
especially when it flatters us.

**24.5 Seek the disconfirming case.** Before committing, actively look for the evidence that we
are wrong.

**24.6 Defer to the person closest to the problem** unless there is a specific reason not to.

**24.7 Being corrected is not a loss of standing** — publicly changing your mind on evidence is
the behaviour we most want to see repeated.

**24.8 Beware conclusions that happen to be profitable.** Any analysis whose result increases our
revenue deserves a second, sceptical review by someone else.

## ARTICLE 25 — GLOBAL EXPANSION PRINCIPLES

**25.1 Enter a market only where we can serve to the standard of this document.** Presence without
capability damages both the person and the company.

**25.2 Local law is the floor; this Constitution is the standard.** Where local practice permits
something these articles forbid, these articles govern. Where local law is stricter, local law
governs.

**25.3 Never adopt a local norm that conflicts with Article 5,** including where competitors treat
it as customary.

**25.4 Hire locally and listen.** People who understand a market's fears and constraints outrank
imported assumptions.

**25.5 One standard of honesty everywhere.** No market receives a weaker version of our disclosure,
pricing transparency, or data protection.

**25.6 Expand along the individual's journey before expanding along the map.** New capability for
existing members generally outranks new geography.

**25.7 Political neutrality, human clarity.** We work with governments and institutions across
political contexts without partisanship, and without compromising the safety or dignity of the
people we serve.

## ARTICLE 26 — INTERNAL CULTURE

**26.1 It must be safe to say no.** A useful health metric: how junior was the last person who
refused a bad instruction?
**26.2 Blameless on honest error; unforgiving on concealment.** Mistakes are tuition. Hiding them
is theft.
**26.3 Systems, not heroes.** If it worked only because someone stayed until 2am, it does not work.
**26.4 Written by default** — decisions, reasoning and dissent recorded.
**26.5 No status theatre.** Titles do not win arguments; evidence does.
**26.6 Rest is operational.** Tired people give poor advice to families making irreversible
decisions.
**26.7 Where we come from is an asset.** This company began in Bihar and works between India, Nepal
and Europe. We do not perform a different origin to be taken seriously.

## ARTICLE 27 — FOUNDER RESPONSIBILITIES

**27.1 Guard Articles 5 and 6** — including against your own future self, your board, and your
investors.
**27.2 Model the cost.** The founder should be the most visible example of a principle being
honoured at a price.
**27.3 Take the reduction before the team does.**
**27.4 Be publicly correctable.** People must see the founder lose an argument to evidence, or
nobody will risk raising one.
**27.5 Own the Founder Letter personally.** It is not written by marketing and is amended only by
the founder.
**27.6 Make yourself operationally unnecessary** within five years.
**27.7 One set of numbers.** The investor story and the public story use the same verifiable
figures. Divergence is a governance failure, not a presentation choice.
**27.8 Appoint a successor to this Constitution,** not merely to the role.

## ARTICLE 28 — EMPLOYEE RESPONSIBILITIES

**28.1 You hold standing authority to advise someone not to proceed.** No approval required; you
will not be second-guessed for it.
**28.2 You must refuse an instruction that breaches Article 5 or 6.** Compliance is not a defence.
**28.3 You must escalate** — to your lead, then the founder, then the board. If all three fail to
act, you are released from confidentiality in respect of that breach.
**28.4 You must document advice given.** If it is not written, it did not happen.
**28.5 You must say "I don't know."**
**28.6 You must challenge the founder when the evidence warrants it.** This is a duty.
**28.7 You must protect the record** as you would your own family's documents.
**28.8 You must keep your knowledge current** (Article 21.1).

## ARTICLE 29 — WHAT RICHENQUEST WILL NEVER BECOME

- An organisation whose advice quietly follows its commercial interests
- A business that sells personal data or attention
- A publisher of figures it cannot evidence
- A holder of undisclosed conflicts of interest
- A brand that manufactures urgency to close
- An operator of automated systems that decide people's futures without a named human accountable
- A company that makes leaving expensive by holding someone's own record
- An organisation that keeps this document on display while no longer practising it

**If RichenQuest becomes any of these, the honest act is to say so and withdraw this document.
Displaying it while breaching it is the single outcome this Constitution cannot survive.**

## ARTICLE 30 — THE TEN-YEAR VISION (2036)

- A first destination for people across South Asia considering an international education or
  career — chosen because our judgement is trusted, not because we outspent anyone.
- **The Career Record** is real, portable, individually owned, and recognised by institutions and
  employers across regions.
- Revenue is diversified across advisory services, institutional products, employer and research
  partnerships — **all operating under Article 5 governance.**
- We publish annual outcomes: where people are one, three and five years afterwards, including
  the disappointments.
- Our disclosure register and steering audits are a reference other organisations copy.
- The alumni community is a primary source of new members, through referral and mentorship.
- We serve more than students: professionals, researchers and their families, without a rewrite of

## ARTICLE 32 — FIRST PRINCIPLES BEHIND EVERY BUSINESS DECISION

When a new situation has no precedent, derive from these.

**32.1 Incentives determine behaviour; intentions do not.** Never rely on people being good —
design so that the aligned action is also the rewarded one. Every structural question reduces to:
*what will this incentive produce in five years?*

**32.2 Information asymmetry is our product.** Our value is closing the gap between what the seller
knows and the individual knows. Anything that widens that gap destroys the company, whatever it earns.

**32.3 Trust compounds; attention decays.** Purchased attention stops the moment spending stops.
Trust accrues and is inherited by every future product.

**32.4 The scarce asset is belief, not capital.** Others can raise more money at any time. They
cannot buy having been demonstrably right, in public, for a decade.

**32.5 Irreversibility deserves disproportionate caution.** A person's year, savings, and
immigration history do not roll back. Weight downside above upside.

**32.6 Verify, then state.** Evidence precedes claim, always, in that order.

**32.7 Optimise the lifetime, not the transaction.** A person is worth far more to this company
across forty years than any single engagement — and that is only true if we behave as though it is.

**32.8 Constraints are strategy.** What we decline to do is what makes us distinguishable. An
organisation that will do anything stands for nothing.

**32.9 Survive.** None of the above matters if the company does not exist. Durability is a
precondition for integrity, not an alternative to it.
