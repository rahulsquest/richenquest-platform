## 3. Part-time earnings — and the rule that must go with them

**Researched this cycle. The numbers are large enough to be dangerous.**

| Country | Regime | Indicative gross earnings |
|---|---|---|
| **Czechia** | **No separate work permit** for students in accredited degree programmes — study status is the basis for employment. Typical student wage **CZK 130–200/hr** | **€342–702/month** ≈ ₹3.9–8.0 L/yr |
| **Poland** | **⚠️ CHANGED — see §4.** Minimum wage **PLN 30.50/hr**; 20 hrs/week term-time for non-listed universities | ~€610/month ≈ ₹7.0 L/yr |

**Arithmetically, a Czech student working 20 hrs/week at mid-range wages out-earns the entire cost of
their degree.**

> ### That number must never be said to a family, and here is the rule.
>
> **The family must be able to fund the GROSS cost. Earnings are a buffer, not a plan.**
>
> The arithmetic assumes work is found immediately and continuously for two years, ignores tax and
> social contributions, ignores that most student jobs require the local language, and ignores the
> effect on grades. **A family that budgets on assumed earnings and then does not find work has a
> student stranded abroad.**
>
> **"Your son can study free in Europe" would be the single most damaging sentence this company could
> produce.** It is now on `qualityGate`'s watchlist logic in spirit; the founder should confirm the
> exact wording to ban (Q1).

**What earnings legitimately change:** they reduce *risk*, not the *requirement*. A family at ₹14 L
against a ₹11.7 L gross cost has a thin margin; the same family with a working student has a real one.
**That is how it should be counselled.**

## 4. 🔴 Poland's work rules tightened — and it damages the Poland thesis

**A material regulatory change I did not previously know about:**

- From **1 December 2025**, a non-EU student may work **without a work permit only if their university is on a Minister of the Interior approved list.**
- **Most Polish universities — public and private — are not on that list.**
- From **1 July 2026** *(already past)*, employers must obtain work permits for students from non-listed universities.

**Vistula's status on that list is UNKNOWN**, and it changes what Poland is worth. File 59 named
Poland the affordability flagship partly on the strength of student earning potential. **If Vistula is
not listed, that leg is materially weaker** — and Czechia's no-permit regime becomes the better
affordability story.

**This has been added to the Vistula email as a fourth question.** It costs nothing to ask and it
changes the recommendation.
