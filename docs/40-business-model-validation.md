# File 40 — Business model validation

Written before the first paying student, which is the only time this analysis is cheap.

**Evidence standard:** every competitor figure and commission rate below is from published sources.
Costs and conversion rates are **labelled estimates** — RichenQuest has no operating data yet.
Currency conversions use **≈ ₹95/€** and are marked `≈`.

## 1. The competitive landscape — verified

| Category | Who | Student fee | How they earn |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free (commission-funded)** | IDP, AECC, Leap Scholar (core counselling) | **₹0** | University commission |
| **Hybrid** | Leap Scholar | ₹0 core | Commission + paid test prep |
| **Paid, mid-market** | Bluesky | ₹30,000–75,000 | Student fee |
| **Paid, upper** | Yocket | ₹60,000–1,20,000 | Student fee |
| **Paid, market ceiling** | quoted range | up to ₹1,50,000 | Student fee |
| **RichenQuest** | — | **₹1,80,000** | Student fee (commission sought) |

**Commission rates, verified:** typically **10–15% of first-year tuition**, reaching **20–25%** for
shorter programmes, **tiered upward by volume** (e.g. bumped at 20/50/100 students per intake), paid
on **enrolment**, on **first year only**. ~97% of agents are paid this way.

**Tuition at RichenQuest's actual pipeline** — private German universities: **€5,000–25,000/year**
bachelor, **€10,000–40,000** master. Gisma quotes up to €15,000 bachelor / €25,000 master.

### The arithmetic that reframes everything

```
Private European programme, €12,000/year tuition
Commission at 12%           =  €1,440   ≈  ₹1,36,000
```

> ### IDP and AECC earn roughly ₹1.35 lakh per student — invisibly.
> ### RichenQuest asks the student for ₹1.80 lakh — visibly.

**The free model is not charity and it is not a loss-leader. It is economically equivalent to
RichenQuest's fee, collected from the other side of the table.** The student experiences it as free
because the cost is embedded in tuition they were paying anyway.

This single fact governs everything that follows.

**Note the destination dependency:** commission on a **public German university (≈€0 tuition) is
≈₹0.** RichenQuest's pipeline is almost entirely *private* institutions, which is commercially
correct — but it means the model only works for students who can afford private tuition, a
narrower segment than "students who want Germany".

## 2. Why would anyone pay RichenQuest instead of using IDP?

Answered honestly, because the honest answer is the useful one.

### Today: there is no compelling reason, and here is exactly what is missing

| What would justify a premium | RichenQuest's verified position |
|---|---|
| Brand and scale | **None.** IDP is global and decades old |
| Public reviews | **Zero.** The single biggest fixable gap |
| Partnerships | **None signed** — cannot be claimed |
| Published success/visa rates | **Not measured** — must not be claimed |
| Placement evidence | **15 verified**, records available |
| Track record | Since **2024**; 1,000+ students **guided** |
| Locality | **Real** — Patna, serving Bihar/Jharkhand/eastern UP/Nepal |
| Independence | **Structurally real, currently unclaimed** |

**A student comparing RichenQuest to IDP today sees: no reviews, no partnerships, no published
outcomes, a two-year history — and a fee 20% above the paid market's ceiling versus free.**

That is not a positioning problem. It is a **value-evidence problem**, and it has three fixable
causes:

1. **Zero reviews.** Ten genuine Google reviews would do more than any argument. Free.
2. **Independence is never stated.** The one structural advantage over IDP/AECC is unarticulated.
3. **The written consultation summary** — already promised in the welcome email — is a demonstration
   of competence rather than a claim of it, and nothing in the sales process leads with it.

### The one defensible answer, stated plainly

> "IDP is paid by the universities it recommends. We are paid by you. If the right university for
> you pays us nothing, that is still the one we will tell you about."

**This is true, verifiable, and structurally uncopiable by a commission-funded competitor.** It is
also **destroyed the moment RichenQuest takes undisclosed commission** — which is why Phase 3 of the
previous mission and Phase 4 of this one are in direct tension.

## 3. Model comparison

| | **A · Fee only** | **B · Commission only** | **C · Hybrid, disclosed** | **D · Premium advisory, commission rebated** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Student pays | ₹1,80,000 | ₹0 | ₹60–90k | ₹1,20–1,50,000 |
| Revenue/student | ₹1.80L | ≈₹0.95L avg¹ | ≈₹2.10L | ₹1.20–1.50L |
| **Ethics** | **Strongest** — no conflict | Weakest — conflict is inherent | Strong **if genuinely disclosed** | **Strongest** — conflict removed *and* refunded |
| **Trust** | High, but must overcome "why not free?" | Market-normal | High if disclosure is real; fragile if it reads as a loophole | **Highest** — the rebate is proof |
| **Profitability/student** | High | Moderate, destination-dependent | **Highest** | High |
| **Scalability** | Limited — price resists volume | **Best** — free removes the biggest objection | Good | Limited — premium is a niche |
| **Cash flow** | **Best** — paid at signature | **Worst** — paid on *enrolment*, 6–12 months later | Good — fee now, commission later | Good |
| **Perception** | "Expensive vs free" | "Same as everyone" | "Cheaper than premium, honest about why" | "The one that doesn't take kickbacks" |
| **Compliance** | Simplest | Standard | **Disclosure must be real, written and per-student** | Simplest, plus a refund mechanism to administer |
| **Differentiation** | Independence — if stated | **None** | Moderate | **Strongest and hardest to copy** |

¹ Blended: ~70% private European placements at ≈₹1.36L, remainder low/zero-commission destinations.

### The cash-flow point that decides more than the ethics

**Model B pays on enrolment, not signature.** A student signed in August enrols the following
September; the commission may land 12+ months after the work is done. **A company with no capital
cannot fund 12 months of counselor salaries against receivables that arrive next year.**

Model A collects at signature. That is not a minor operational nuance — **for an unfunded company it
is the difference between surviving to student 50 and not.**

### Why "fee + undisclosed commission" is not on this list

₹1.80L fee + ₹1.36L commission ≈ **₹3.16L per student — more than double the paid market's
ceiling**, while claiming independence. It is the highest-revenue option and it is the one that ends
the company's reputation when a student discovers it. It is excluded deliberately.

## 6. Recommendation

**Optimising for long-term trust, brand, sustainable profit and differentiation — not maximum
revenue — the recommendation is Model D, entered through a staged path.**

### Model D · Premium advisory, commission disclosed and rebated

Charge the student **₹1,20,000–1,50,000**. Where a university pays commission, **disclose it and
credit it against the student's fee.**

**Why D, on the evidence:**

1. **It is the only model where the differentiator survives contact with the commission line.**
   A and D both permit "we work for you"; only D keeps it true after partnerships are signed — which
   is the stated Phase 3 goal. C's disclosure is honest but reads as a discount; **D's rebate is
   proof.**
2. **It prices inside the market.** ₹1.2–1.5L sits at the paid ceiling rather than 20% above it, and
   the rebate makes the net cost lower still — often materially below Yocket while claiming more.
3. **Cash flow works.** Fee at signature funds operations; commission arrives later and reduces the
   *next* student's fee rather than being needed to pay this month's salaries.
4. **It is uncopiable by the market leaders.** IDP and AECC cannot rebate a commission that is their
   only income. This is a genuine structural moat, not a marketing line.
5. **It matches the company's existing discipline.** A business that already refuses to overstate
   claims in its own build pipeline is credible when it says "here is what the university pays us".

**The honest cost of D:** it caps revenue per student below C, and it requires administering rebates.
That is the price of the moat. **Optimising for revenue would choose C; optimising for trust chooses
D**, and the brief said trust.

### The staged path — do not start at D

**Stage 1 · now → first 10 students.** Charge **₹1,20,000** flat. No commission exists yet, so the
rebate is theoretical. **The goal of these ten students is not profit — it is reviews, evidence and
a real conversion rate.** Ten reviews and a measured conversion rate are worth more than the
₹6 lakh of extra fee that pricing at ₹1.8L would theoretically capture and probably would not.

**Stage 2 · 10 → 50 students.** Add partnerships. **Disclose and rebate commission from the first
signed agreement.** Raise the fee to ₹1,50,000 only once reviews and evidenced outcomes exist to
support it.

**Stage 3 · 50+.** With volume, commission tiers improve (verified: rates step up at 20/50/100
students per intake). The rebate becomes a widening, visible advantage — the more successful the
agency, the cheaper it is for the student. **That is a compounding position no competitor can match
without abandoning their own economics.**

### Three assumptions of yours that the evidence challenges

1. **"₹1 crore at 100 students."** At ₹1.8L it is 56; at the recommended ₹1.2L it is 83. **100
   students is a ₹1–1.8 crore business** depending on a price that is not yet set.
2. **"Premium fee is the model."** ₹1,80,000 is above every published paid competitor while the
   company has **zero reviews, zero partnerships and no published outcomes.** Premium pricing is
   earned with evidence, and the evidence does not exist yet. **Price low, gather evidence, raise.**
3. **"Partnerships and student fees are complementary."** They are in tension. Taking commission
   while charging a premium for independence is the one combination that destroys the only
   defensible advantage. **D resolves it; nothing else does.**

### What must be decided before the first student

| # | Decision | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The price** | It is in every conversation from lead one, and changing it later devalues early students |
| 2 | **Commission stance** | Determines what Wave 1 asks universities, and whether the independence claim can be made at all |
| 3 | **Refund policy** | Not researched here and **not published by most competitors** — a written, honest refund term is a cheap, real trust signal nobody else offers |

### What would prove this recommendation wrong

Stated so the model is falsifiable rather than merely persuasive:

- If the first 20 losses cite **`Lost_Reason: Budget`** at ₹1.2L, price is still too high — the
  market is more price-sensitive than assumed and B deserves reconsideration.
- If students say the independence argument does not register, the moat is theoretical and D loses
  its rationale.
- If commission rates negotiated are below ~8%, the rebate is too small to be persuasive and D
  collapses into A.

**All three are measurable within the first 30 students.** The platform already captures every field
required — `Lost_Reason`, `Deals.Amount`, `Lead_Source_Detail`, case `Stage`. **Nothing needs
building. It needs using.**
