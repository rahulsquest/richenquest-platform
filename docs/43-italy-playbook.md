## 1. The two findings that change the Italy pitch

### 1.1 Italy accepts loans and scholarships as proof of funds. Germany does not.

| | Germany | Italy |
|---|---|---|
| Amount | **€11,904** blocked account | **sources conflict** — see below |
| Form | **must be deposited cash** in a blocked account | bank statements, **sponsorship, scholarships, or approved education loans** |

**The form matters more than the amount.** A Bihar family that cannot deposit ~₹11 lakh in cash
*can* often produce a sanctioned education loan. **Italy has a route for families Germany excludes** —
and that, not the headline figure, is the accessibility argument.

> ⚠️ **The Italian figure is genuinely unsettled across sources.** Reported: MUR reference
> **€534.41/month × 13 = €6,947.33/yr**; also **€10,179.85/yr**; also **€5,520–6,072/yr**.
> **Never quote a single number to a family.** The requirement varies by consulate and year —
> **confirm with the specific Italian consulate handling the application, every intake.**

### 1.2 The Cutro Decree exempts students from the Decreto Flussi quota

Italy normally rations non-EU work permits through the **Decreto Flussi** annual quota. Under the
Cutro Decree reforms, **international graduates of Italian universities can convert a student
residence permit into a full-time work permit at any time of year, without waiting for the quota.**

There is also a **Permesso di soggiorno per attesa occupazione** — a **12-month, non-renewable**
job-search permit after graduation.

**This is the most under-communicated fact in Italian student mobility.** For a family weighing
long-term outcomes, "an Italian degree converts to work authorisation outside the quota lottery" is
worth more than any ranking. **Most consultancies do not explain it because they are not paid to
think past enrolment.**

| **Post-Arrival Add-on** | Residence permit, codice fiscale, bank, first-month support | ₹25,000–40,000 | Students already admitted elsewhere |

**Why the scholarship package is the right flagship:** it targets a €14,000–16,000/yr outcome. A
₹1.2–1.5 lakh fee against ₹13–15 lakh/yr of value is an easy conversation — and it is the one
package no commission-funded competitor will build, because public Italian universities pay them
almost nothing.

**Pricing note:** these are *indicative bands for the founder to set*, not decisions. **F1 remains
open.**

| Brand, scale, reviews | **far stronger** | **stronger** | **weak — 0 reviews** |

**The defensible claim, in one sentence:**

> "Agencies paid commission by universities have little reason to send you to Italy, because Italian
> public universities pay them almost nothing. We are paid by you — so Italy, where the scholarship

**That is true, verifiable, and structurally difficult for a commission-funded competitor to
counter.** It is also consistent with File 40's Model D.

**What it does not fix:** zero reviews and no signed partnerships. **The Italy story is the message;
reviews are still the prerequisite.**

**Two rules for anyone using this file:**

1. **The disputed visa figure and the unverified work-hour rules must not be stated to a student.**

2. **Re-verify annually.** DSU thresholds, visa figures and permit rules all change. A knowledge base
   that is not re-checked becomes a liability rather than an asset.
