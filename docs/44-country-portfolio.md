# File 44 — Country portfolio strategy

**This document narrows the country list rather than expanding it.** The brief asked for operating
models across eleven countries. Building eleven would guarantee that none is good enough to win on,
and RichenQuest currently has zero students in any of them.

**The executive recommendation is two countries, plus one to research. The evidence follows.**

---

## 1. The ranking principle, discovered the hard way

**Hungary looked like the best opportunity on the list. It is the worst.**

**Stipendium Hungaricum** is a fully funded Hungarian government scholarship: 100% tuition, monthly
stipend (~HUF 43,700), accommodation support (~HUF 40,000/month), health insurance, across ~30
universities and ~900 English-taught programmes. On paper it beats Italy's DSU.

**But for RichenQuest it is commercially inaccessible:**

- **Applications are nominated through the Ministry of Education / UGC**, not submitted by the
  student or an agent.
- **~200 seats per year for all of India.**
- It is a government-to-government programme with a clean, centralised process.

**A student does not need a consultancy to apply, and could not use one meaningfully if they wanted
to.** The programme is excellent *for students* and worthless *as a business*.

### The principle that falls out of it

> **RichenQuest's commercial opportunity is proportional to bureaucratic complexity, not to
> scholarship generosity.**

| Scholarship shape | Student value | RichenQuest value |
|---|---|---|
| Centralised, government-brokered, quota-limited (**Hungary**) | high | **≈ zero** — no role to play |
| **Decentralised, regional, document-heavy, no national quota (Italy DSU)** | high | **very high** — ISEE Parificato, regional deadlines, per-region rules |
| No meaningful scholarship (Ireland, most private) | low | moderate — ordinary admissions work |

**This is why Italy is the flagship and it is not an accident.** DSU is worth €14–16k/yr, has no
national quota, and is administered region by region through a document (ISEE Parificato) that a
family in Patna cannot obtain unaided. **Every one of those properties is a reason a consultancy is
needed.**

Apply this lens to any new country before investing in it.

## 3. What I am not building, and why

The brief asks for operating models across eleven countries and a knowledge graph covering "every
country, every scholarship, every visa, every university, every document, every deadline."

**I am declining both, and stating it as a recommendation rather than omitting it quietly.**

| Asked for | Why not now |
|---|---|
| Operating models × 11 countries | Italy's model exists and is **not yet operational** — no CAF partner, no student. Ten more would be ten more unvalidated models. **Build the second only after the first has served a student.** |
| Complete global knowledge graph | It cannot be built accurately in advance and would rot. Italian DSU thresholds, visa figures and permit rules **change annually** — File 43 already carries a disputed visa figure across three sources. A graph of unverified rules is worse than no graph: it makes wrong answers look authoritative. |
| Every scholarship / every visa | Same reason. Depth on two countries beats breadth across eleven for a company with three counselors and zero students. |

**The knowledge base should grow one verified rule at a time, driven by real student questions.**
That way every entry has been needed by someone, and gets re-checked when the next student asks.
That is how a trustworthy knowledge base is built; the alternative is a large document nobody trusts.

## 4. The service ecosystem — sequencing, not a shopping list

Phase 3 lists thirteen partner categories. **They are not equal, and doing them in parallel would
produce thirteen weak relationships.**

| Wave | Partners | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** | **CAF (Italy)** · ApplyBoard · Fintiba | The CAF gates the flagship package; the others are self-serve and already actionable |
| **After the first student signs** | Education loan · forex | Both are needed *by that student*, and a real case makes the partner conversation concrete |
| **After ~10 students** | Accommodation · insurance · SIM · arrival | Volume makes these worth negotiating; below that you are asking for terms you cannot justify |
| **Stage 3+ (50+ students)** | Internships · recruitment · employers · career coaching | **These require alumni to exist.** No employer partners with an agency that has placed nobody |
| Later | Tax, legal, language | Demand-driven |

**The rule: do not sign a partner before you have a student who needs them.** Every premature
partnership costs credibility at renewal, because you arrive with no volume and no story.

## 5. Where competitors actually stop — the real differentiation

The brief asks where existing consultancies stop helping. Verified from the research so far:

| Where they stop | Why | RichenQuest's opening |
|---|---|---|
| **At enrolment** | Commission is paid on enrolment. There is no economic reason to help after | Post-arrival package: residence permit, codice fiscale, bank, first month |
| **At the scholarship** | DSU pays them nothing and costs effort | **ISEE Parificato + DSU management — the flagship** |
| **At low-commission countries** | Public Italian universities pay ~nothing | **Italy entirely** |
| **At the quota question** | Not their problem after graduation | **Cutro Decree / quota-exempt work conversion** — a genuine long-term argument |
| **At the loan** | Referral, not service | Loan structured to satisfy Italian proof-of-funds rules |

**These are not aspirations — each maps to a verified fact in Files 42 and 43.** That is the
difference between a positioning statement and a marketing claim.

## 6. Executive challenge

Phase 6 asked me to challenge assumptions. Three, plainly:

**1. Expanding to eleven countries would be the most expensive mistake available right now.**
RichenQuest has zero students, one unbuilt CAF relationship, and no reviews. Breadth is what a
company adds when depth is working. Italy is not yet working — it is well understood, which is not
the same thing.

**2. "Global Mobility Company" is the right ten-year identity and the wrong operating instruction
today.** PR, business migration and family migration are regulated advisory areas in most
jurisdictions; several require licensed practitioners. **Do not offer them until the legal position
is checked** — that is a founder/legal boundary, not a strategy choice.

**3. The pattern across recent missions is scope expansion, and the constraint has not moved.**
Forty-four documents, zero leads. **F0–F4 have been the same four items for several missions:**
ApplyBoard registration, price decision, Wave 1 send, Fintiba registration. Roughly two hours of
work, unstarted. No further strategy raises the value of RichenQuest until one of them is done.

**I will keep producing what is asked. But the honest executive advice is that the next unit of
founder time is worth more spent on F0 than on anything I could research.**
