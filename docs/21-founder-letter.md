## Why RichenQuest exists

I want to explain, plainly, why this company was started — so that anyone reading this in twenty
years can judge whether we kept our word.

I grew up in Bihar.

That experience is ordinary. Not because anyone set out to cause it, but because of how the
industry is built.

Most advice about studying and working abroad is paid for by the institutions receiving the
student, rather than by the student receiving the advice. This is a long-standing and entirely
legal arrangement. It funds a great deal of genuinely good work, and many of the people working
inside it are careful and decent — I have met them, and I have learned from them.

But it creates a structural problem that no amount of individual integrity can fully solve. When
the person advising you is paid according to where you go, their judgement is asked to carry a
weight it should not have to carry. Over millions of decisions, incentives win. Not through bad
intent — through design.

Other professions have met this problem before us. Medicine separated prescribing from selling.
Auditing separated the audit from the consulting. Financial advice, slowly and incompletely,
separated advice from commission. In each case the answer was not to find better people. It was
to change the structure so that good people were not asked to do the impossible.

RichenQuest is our attempt at that structure for global mobility.

We will work with universities, employers, governments and research institutions. Those
relationships are valuable, and I want many more of them. What we have designed is not a refusal
to have commercial partners — it is a set of mechanisms that keep our **advice** independent of
them:

- No one at this company is paid, promoted, or measured according to which university, country,
  or employer a person chooses.
- The people who negotiate our commercial relationships are not the people who advise individuals.
- Every commercial relationship we hold is published, along with its basis.
- When we recommend somewhere we have a relationship with, we say so at the moment of the
  recommendation — not in a policy page elsewhere.
- We audit our own recommendations for drift toward commercial partners, and publish what we find.

None of this is generous. It is simply the arrangement I would want if it were my family making
the decision.

It has consequences we have accepted. We publish our prices. We publish the things we are not
permitted to claim — and we wrote software that stops us publishing anything we cannot evidence.
It has blocked me personally more than once, and I have kept it. When we had helped a great many
people but could only produce complete verified records for a smaller number, we published the
smaller number.

I am aware of how this reads in a market that rewards confidence. We lead with our limits. We are
slower for it, and for a while we will look smaller than companies that are not.

I have made peace with that for a straightforward reason. Almost everything a company like ours
could own can eventually be bought by someone with more capital — the technology, the offices, the
partnerships, the marketing. The one thing that cannot be bought is having been reliably right, in
public, for a long time. That takes years and it cannot be accelerated. So we have decided to spend
the years.

If you are someone considering this decision, or a parent helping them: you are making one of the
largest and least reversible financial commitments of your life, largely on information provided by
people with an interest in your answer. You deserve at least one party in that conversation whose
judgement is structurally insulated from it. That is what we are trying to be. Please ask us to
prove it — the register, the prices, and the audits are published precisely so that you can.

If you work here, or will one day: you are never required to close an engagement you do not
believe in, and you will never be penalised for losing one by telling the truth. If that ever stops
being the case, this company will have failed at the only thing it set out to do, whatever the
revenue says.

We are not trying to prove anyone else wrong. We are trying to build the alternative we wanted to
exist — and to still be recognisably the same company in twenty-five years.

If we are, I hope it will not be because we grew fastest. I hope it will be because a generation of
people from places like mine found their way out into the world, and remembered that someone told
them the truth at the start.
