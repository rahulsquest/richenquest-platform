## 2. Who should choose Debrecen

**All four should be true:**

1. **Master's, February or September 2027.** Direct Bachelor's does not exist in February.
2. **No IELTS certificate, or a band at 5.5.** *This is the actual reason to choose Debrecen.* The interview route is the strongest access story in the portfolio.
3. **A study gap of up to 4 years** — India and Nepal are not on the restricted list.
4. **Family can fund ₹21–23 L, or has confirmed a dormitory place.**

**The ideal student is one whom other consultancies have already turned away** — no test, a gap, and
a real budget. Debrecen says yes where the market says no. **That is the product.**

## 3. Who should NOT choose Debrecen

| Profile | Why |
|---|---|
| **Total budget ₹10–15 L** | **DO NOT RECOMMEND.** Realistic total is ₹20.5–22.5 L. Point them to a 2-yr Master's in Czechia/Poland, or Italy for September |
| **Wants a direct Bachelor's in February** | Does not exist. Preparatory route or September |
| **Holds IELTS 6.5+ and a strong academic record** | **They are paying for access they do not need.** Debrecen's advantage is admission flexibility — a strong applicant should be shopping on cost and outcomes instead |
| **Needs a scholarship to make it work at all** | Merit discount starts **Year 2**. Year 1 must be funded in full. Stipendium is ~200 seats for all of India |
| **Cannot risk USD 500** | Non-refundable before any decision |
| **Wants guaranteed accommodation** | The university does not guarantee it to self-financed students |

## 4. When it must NEVER be recommended

1. **When the family's budget is stated as ₹10–15 lakh.** Recommending it is selling a course they cannot finish.
2. **Before the accommodation question is answered**, unless the family is told plainly that the total may be ₹5.76 L higher than the best case.
3. **On employability grounds.** We have researched none.
4. **As "the affordable option."** It is the *access* option. File 59 established this; §0 confirms it.

## 7. Could a competitor reproduce this in a day?

**The facts, yes.** The tuition, the deadline, the dorm rates are all public.

**The structure, no** — and not because it is hard. **A commission-funded agency cannot publish §3 or
§4.** *"Do not recommend this to a ₹10–15 lakh family"* and *"a strong applicant is paying for access
they do not need"* are sentences that reduce enrolments at a university they are paid by.

> **The moat is not the research. It is the willingness to publish who should say no.**
