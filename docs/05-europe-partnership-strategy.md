## 1. The Germany commission trap (read this first)

**German public universities do not pay recruitment commissions.** They're tuition-free or near-free, admissions are centralized (often via uni-assist), and they have no budget or mechanism to pay agents. Sending them partnership emails wastes your outreach engine.

So Germany splits into **two revenue lanes**, and your system runs both:

### Lane A — Commission lane: German PRIVATE universities + other European institutions
Germany has ~115 state-recognised private universities charging roughly €8,000–40,000+ per year — and these actively recruit through agents, run multiple intakes per year, and are built for international students. This is where partnership commissions live in Germany. Ireland is the other strong European commission market (English-speaking, universities routinely work with agents). Netherlands/France/Poland/Baltics have selected agent-friendly institutions.

### Lane B — Service-fee lane: German PUBLIC universities
Students *want* public universities (free tuition). You can't earn commission from the university — so you earn from the **student side**: your counseling/application service packages, APS guidance, uni-assist application support, SOP/LOR services, visa filing. Plus **affiliate income nobody tells new consultancies about**: blocked-account providers (Fintiba, Expatrio, Coracle), German health insurers, and student accommodation platforms all run referral/partner programs for agents. Every Germany-bound student needs a blocked account and insurance regardless of university — that's recurring affiliate revenue with zero extra work once you're enrolled in their partner programs. (Action: apply to Fintiba/Expatrio partner programs in Week 4 — I'll draft those applications.)

**Positioning bonus:** advertising "we also place students in FREE German public universities" is a powerful trust signal and lead magnet — competitors who only push commission-paying privates look biased. You monetize Lane B students through service fees while Lane A pays commissions. Counselors must always disclose honestly which option fits the *student* best; long-term reputation is the business.

## 2. One conversation, many universities: education groups

Several German privates belong to **Global University Systems (GUS)** — including BSBI, Gisma, and University of Europe for Applied Sciences. Groups like this manage agent relationships centrally, so **one signed agreement can unlock multiple institutions at once**. Your outreach should target the *group's* regional/India partnerships team as a priority track, alongside individual universities. (Same logic applies later to other groups and to Ireland's college groups.)
