# File 56 — Reality check, and one email

**Two instructions in this brief are aimed at me, and both are fair.**

*"Don't repeat the same recommendation forever — break it into the smallest executable step."* I have
said *"send the three emails"* in five consecutive reports. Saying it a sixth time would be me
managing my own to-do list, not yours.

*"What assumption are we making that could be wrong?"* Here are mine, challenged.

---

## 1. The assumption I got wrong: what February is actually for

**I have been telling you February 2027 will produce 8–15 enrolments. I no longer believe that.**

Run the honest arithmetic. Today is 16 August. Zero leads exist. Documents must be complete by early
October. That leaves ~7 weeks to find families, counsel them, and collect transcripts, passports and
funds evidence from first-generation applicants who in many cases **do not yet have a passport**.

> **A realistic February outcome is 2–5 students. Not 8–15.**

**And it does not matter, because I was measuring the wrong thing.**

| What I said February was for | What it is actually for |
|---|---|
| 8–15 enrolments | **Getting from 0 to 1** |
| Revenue this quarter | **The first review, the first testimonial, the first case study** |
| Hitting O2's target | **Proving the funnel works before September, when it has to work at scale** |

**RichenQuest's real risk is not a small February. It is a zero February — because zero students means
zero reviews, and zero reviews is the gap File 43 §4 named as the biggest weakness against IDP and
AECC.** September 2027 has 45 weeks, Italy, Germany and DSU. That is the volume intake. **February's
job is to produce the first three students whose stories make September work.**

**This changes the target, not the plan.** Six seminars is still right. But if it yields 3 students
instead of 12, that is **success**, and it should not be reported as a miss.

## 3. Why the emails have not gone — and the smallest step

**I do not think you are too busy. Three emails is fifteen minutes.**

**I think the uncomfortable part is the reply.** A university asks *"how many students will you send
us this year?"* and the honest answer today is *"none yet."* That is an awkward sentence to write to
an institution you want to be taken seriously by — and an ambiguous non-email keeps the door
pleasantly unresolved.

**So: one email. Not three.**

### → Send Debrecen only. Today.

**Why Debrecen and not the others:**

- It is the **only university that can produce a February 2027 student**. The other two cannot affect this intake.
- It is **one field away from READY** — commission status. Nothing else is missing.
- It is an **existing relationship**, so the email is a continuation, not a cold approach.

**The other two wait until Debrecen replies.** Then you will know how these conversations actually go
and the second email will be better. That is the point of doing them in sequence.

### The answer to the question you are worried about

If they ask how many students you will send:

> *"We're a new company — incorporated in January 2026 — so I'm not going to quote you a number I
> can't stand behind. What I can tell you is that we recruit from Bihar, Jharkhand, eastern Uttar
> Pradesh and Nepal, which the large Indian agencies serve thinly, and that we've already built a
> verified profile of your February requirements because your IELTS 5.5 and interview route fit our
> students better than almost anything else in Europe. I'd rather start small and be accurate than
> promise volume and miss."*

**That answer is stronger than a number.** International offices are told inflated projections
constantly; almost nobody says *"I won't quote you a figure I can't stand behind."* It is the same
positioning as everything else RichenQuest does, applied to a partner instead of a family.

**And if they say they don't work with agents:** that is a useful answer, not a bad one. You continue
supporting students through the standard route — which the email already asks about as question 2 —
and you stop waiting on a commission that was never coming. **A confirmed no unblocks the F1 pricing
decision (File 54 §2). An unanswered question does not.**
