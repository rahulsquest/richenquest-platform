# RichenQuest — Counsellor Handbook v1.0

## 1 · The job

You are not selling a course. You are managing the largest financial decision most of
these families will ever make, on a timeline they cannot see and you can.

**Three rules that outrank everything else in this handbook.**

1. **Never say a number you have not verified.** "I will check and tell you today" is
   always acceptable. A confident wrong number is how families lose money.
2. **Never promise an outcome.** Admission is the university's decision. A visa is a
   government's. No fee changes either.
3. **Say the hard thing early.** A family told in August that February is unreachable is
   disappointed. A family told in December has lost a year and their deposit.

## 2 · Before every call

Open `student360()`. Not the CRM record. It gives you the state, the blockers, the next
irreversible date and the one action that matters, with the reasoning attached.

**You may not ask a question the wizard already answered.** It is in the file. Asking
again tells the family nobody read it.

## 3 · Counselling script

**Opening (2 min)**
> "Thank you for the detail you sent — I've read all of it, so I won't ask you to repeat
> anything. I want to spend this call on three things: what you actually want, what it
> will really cost, and whether the timing works. Is your father or mother able to join?"

**Discovery (10 min)** — see §4.

**The cost conversation (10 min)** — this is the call's centre of gravity.
> "Most agencies will quote you a tuition fee. I'm going to give you the whole number,
> because that's what you'll actually spend. For [country], for one year, that's tuition,
> rent, food, insurance, the visa, the residence permit and your flight. Here's where each
> figure comes from and when I checked it."

**Timing (5 min)** — always backwards from the start date.
> "Your course starts in February. Working backwards: the visa needs roughly [N] days, so
> the file has to be filed by [date]. That means the offer by [date], the application by
> [date], documents by [date]. That's why I keep asking about your passport."

**Risks (5 min)** — name at least one real one.
> "The honest risk in your profile is [X]. It isn't fatal, and here's what we do about it."

**Close (3 min)**
> "Two things from you this week: [documents], and [passport if relevant]. From me:
> your written shortlist with full costs by [date]. And you'll get a written update every
> Friday whether or not anything has moved."

## 4 · Discovery questions

**Motivation** — *Why abroad, and why now?* · *What does this degree need to lead to?* ·
*Have you applied anywhere before?* (a prior refusal changes everything)

**Money** — *Who is paying, and is that money available now or being arranged?* ·
*If the total came to ₹5 lakh more than you expect, does this still happen?* ·
*Is a loan involved, and has it been sanctioned or only discussed?*

**Timing** — *Is February a preference or a requirement?* · *What happens if you wait
until September?* · *Is anyone at home against this?*

**Constraints** — *Any medical condition, family obligation or court matter we should
know about?* · *Any gap in your education we will need to explain?*

**The question that saves cases:** *"Is there anything you're worried I'll judge you
for?"* Backlogs, gaps, a refusal, a father's income — the things families hide are exactly
the things that sink applications later.

## 5 · Objection handling

**"Another agency quoted less."**
> "They probably quoted tuition. Ask them for the whole number including rent, insurance,
> the blocked account and the permit. If it's genuinely lower, take it — and let me tell
> you what to check in their paperwork first."

**"Can you guarantee the visa?"**
> "No. Nobody can, and anyone who says otherwise is lying to you. What I can tell you is
> what visa officers refuse people for, and make sure none of it is true of your file."

**"Why pay you at all?"**
> "You don't have to. If you want to apply yourself, I'll tell you which universities to
> look at and you can go. What you're paying for is that we've worked backwards from your
> start date and we'll tell you when something is about to go wrong."

**"My cousin's agent got him a scholarship."**
> "Scholarships exist and we'll apply for every one you're eligible for. But we plan your
> budget assuming you get none. If one arrives it's a relief, not a plan."

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Of course. One thing that shouldn't wait: apply for your passport this week. It takes
> weeks, it costs little, and it's useful whatever you decide."

## 6 · True cost — how to explain it

Say it as a stack, not a number:

> Tuition · Rent · Food and daily living · Health insurance · Blocked account or proof of
> funds (returned to you, but you must have it) · Visa fee · Residence permit fee ·
> Flights · One-time setup (deposit, bedding, SIM, registration)

**Verified example — Debrecen, Hungary.** The university publishes a living cost of
**USD 800/month**. Against that published figure, the realistic first-year total is
**₹30.3–31.2 lakh**. *(Source: university's own published figure. Re-verify before quoting
— it changes.)*

This estimate was revised upward four times during research. Say that to families: it is
the strongest evidence that we correct ourselves rather than defend a first guess.

## 10 · Scholarships — what you may say

**Permitted:** "You meet the stated criteria for this scheme." · "The deadline is [date],
from [source] checked [date]." · "Decisions are usually announced around [month], which may
be after your deposit is due."

**Never:** any probability, chance or likelihood of award · "you should get" · a budget
that only works if a scholarship arrives · a scheme not verified on the awarding body's
own page.

## 11 · Visa — what you may say

**Permitted:** the documents the mission's own published list requires · the fee, from the
mission's page · that appointment availability is unpredictable and is the least
controllable part of the process · that a refusal has grounds and those grounds decide
what happens next.

**Never:** a success rate · a processing time as a promise · "they always approve students
from X" · any offer to book, sign, complete, or attend on the student's behalf.
