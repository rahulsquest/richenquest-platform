# SECTION 3 · Counselling Scripts

Read `student360()` before every call. **Never ask a question the wizard already answered.**

---

## 3.1 First Call — 20 minutes

**Open (2 min)**
> "{{first_name}}? {{counsellor}} from RichenQuest — you sent us an application on
> {{date}}, reference {{case_no}}. I've read all of it, so I won't make you repeat
> anything. Is now still alright, and is your {{parent_relation}} nearby?"

**Frame (1 min)**
> "Three things in twenty minutes: what you actually want, what it will really cost, and
> whether your timing works. If any of those don't add up, I'll tell you today rather than
> in three months."

**Confirm, don't re-ask (3 min)**
> "You've put {{level}} in {{course}}, starting {{intake}}, budget {{budget}}, and you're
> looking at {{countries}}. Anything changed since you filled that in?"

**Passport — if not held, this is the first substantive topic (2 min)**
> "You said you don't have a passport yet. I want to be direct: that's the single most
> common reason a family misses an intake. It takes weeks. Whatever you decide about us,
> apply this week — it's cheap and it's useful either way."

**The real question (5 min)**
> "Why abroad, and why {{intake}}?"
> "Who's paying — and is that money available now, or being arranged?"
> "If the total came in ₹5 lakh above what you're expecting, does this still happen?"

**Cost preview (4 min)**
> "I won't give you a full number today — I'd rather check it than guess. But so you know
> the shape: for Hungary, one year, everything in — tuition, rent, food, insurance, visa,
> permit, flight — you're looking at roughly ₹30 lakh. Most agencies will quote you the
> tuition, which is about a fifth of that."

**Close (3 min)**
> "Next: I'll send you a written shortlist with the full cost of each option and where every
> figure comes from, by {{date}}. From you this week: {{top_2_documents}}. And you'll get a
> written update every Friday, whether or not there's news. Fair?"

---

## 3.2 Follow-up Call

> "{{first_name}}, {{counsellor}}. You've had the shortlist a few days. Before I ask what
> you think — was anything in it confusing or wrong? I'd rather fix an error than defend it."

*If no decision yet:*
> "That's fine. What's the actual blocker — is it the money, the country, or is someone at
> home not convinced? I can help with two of those and I'd rather know which."

*Never:* "Have you decided yet?" — it invites a no.

---

## 3.3 Parent Call

> "Namaste {{parent_name}}. {{counsellor}} from RichenQuest. {{first_name}} has been
> working with us on {{case_no}}. I wanted to speak to you directly, because you're the one
> arranging the money and it's not fair for you to hear the number last."

**The number, first**
> "The total for the first year at {{university}} is ₹{{total}}. That's tuition, rent, food,
> insurance, the visa, the residence permit and the flight. Separately, you'll need to
> *show* ₹{{funds}} in an account — that money comes back to you, but it must exist."

**What you'll be asked to do**
> "Three payments matter: our fee in stages, the university deposit around {{month}}, and
> the proof of funds before the visa. Nothing else is hidden."

**The commitment**
> "Every Friday you'll get a written update from us, same as {{first_name}}. If the timing
> stops working, you'll hear it from me early — not in November."

**If they ask about jobs / PR**
> "I won't promise you that. Work rights depend on rules that change and on how
> {{first_name}} does. What I can tell you is exactly what the current published rule is,
> and I'll show you where it says so."

---

## 3.4 Closing Call

> "{{first_name}}, you've had the proposal for {{days}} days. Let me put the decision in
> front of you plainly.
>
> {{university}}, {{programme}}, starting {{intake}}. Total ₹{{total}}. Our fee is
> ₹{{fee}}, in four stages — today you'd pay ₹{{stage1}}, which is {{pct}}%.
>
> The deadline is {{deadline}}, {{days_left}} days away. Working backwards, documents have
> to be with us by {{docs_by}} for that to hold.
>
> If you want to proceed, I'll send the agreement and the payment link today. If you don't,
> tell me and I'll close your file — no follow-up calls. What would you like to do?"

**Silence after this. Do not fill it.**

*If yes:* "Good. Agreement and link in ten minutes. Please read the refund table — it's
short and it matters."

*If not yet:* "What's the one thing that would make it a yes?"

*If no:* "Understood, and thank you for being straight with me. Two things before I close
it: apply for your passport anyway, and if anyone asks you about studying abroad, the True
Cost report is free — send it to them."

---

## 3.5 Visa Call

> "{{first_name}}, your appointment is {{date}} at {{location}}. Three things.
>
> One — take every document on this list, in this order. I'll send it now.
>
> Two — you will sign your own declarations. Read them. If anything on that form isn't
> accurate, stop and call me before you sign. I will never sign anything for you and
> neither should anyone else.
>
> Three — answer truthfully and briefly. If you don't know, say you don't know. Don't guess
> and don't elaborate. Officers are trained to notice rehearsed answers.
>
> Call me the moment you come out, whatever happened."

---

## 3.6 Bad News

**Timing lost — call, never message**
> "{{first_name}}, I need to be straight with you. The last date we could safely have filed
> your visa for {{intake}} has passed.
>
> I'm not going to tell you it might still work, because it very likely won't and you'd be
> spending money on that hope.
>
> What I want to do is move you to {{next_intake}}. Your documents, your shortlist and any
> offer carry over — the work isn't wasted.
>
> Can I call you and your {{parent_relation}} together this evening?"

**If it was our error:** "This was our mistake, not yours. Our refund policy covers exactly
this and I'm applying it — you'll get your fee back in full. I'd still like to get you
there in {{next_intake}}."

**Visa refused**
> "{{first_name}}, the decision came back and it's a refusal. I'm sorry.
>
> Before anything else — send me a photo of the whole letter, including every reason listed.
> Don't summarise it. I need the exact wording, because refusals split into two very
> different kinds and which one this is decides whether we appeal, reapply, or change
> country.
>
> I'll call you back within the hour. This is not the end of it."

---

## 3.7 Scholarship Discussion

> "You're eligible to apply for {{scheme}} — you meet the stated criteria, and here's the
> page that says so, checked on {{date}}.
>
> Now the part most agencies skip: decisions come around {{month}}, which is **after** your
> deposit is due. So we plan your budget as if you get nothing. If it comes, it's a relief.
> If we planned around it and it didn't come, you'd be stuck in {{month}} with a deposit
> paid and a gap.
>
> I won't tell you your chances. Nobody honestly can."

---

## 3.8 Budget Discussion

> "Let me show you the whole stack rather than one number."
> *(walk the Budget Calculator line by line)*
>
> "The last line is the one that decides this: ₹{{cash}} the family has to actually
> arrange. Everything above it is detail."

**If short:**
> "I'll be honest — your budget is about ₹{{gap}} short of the cheapest option I can
> verify. There are three honest routes: a loan for the difference, a cheaper country once
> I've verified one, or waiting one intake and saving. What there isn't, is a version where
> the number is smaller than it is."

---

## 3.9 Gap Discussion

> "You've got a {{years}} year gap. That's not a problem in itself — European universities
> see it constantly. What matters is the explanation, and we write it now rather than
> improvise it at a visa counter.
>
> What were you actually doing? Working, family responsibility, health, retaking exams?"

> "Good. Then we say exactly that, plainly, with any evidence you have. What sinks a gap is
> a vague answer, not a long one."

---

## 3.10 IELTS Discussion

> "Before you spend ₹17,000 on IELTS — does your college issue a Medium of Instruction
> letter? Many European universities accept it, it's usually free, and it takes a week.
>
> Let me confirm whether {{university}} accepts it for {{programme}}. If they do, don't book
> the test. If they don't, we'll book it now so the date doesn't become your bottleneck."

## 3.11 Objection Bank

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Another agency quoted less." | "They quoted tuition. Ask them for the total including rent, insurance, proof of funds and the permit. If it's genuinely lower, take it — but let me tell you what to check in their paperwork first." |
| "Can you guarantee the visa?" | "No. Nobody can, and anyone who says they can is lying to you. What I can do is make sure nothing in your file gives them a reason to refuse." |
| "Why should I pay you at all?" | "You don't have to. I'll tell you which universities to look at and you can apply yourself. What you're paying for is that we work backwards from your start date and tell you when something is about to go wrong." |
| "My cousin's agent got a scholarship." | "We'll apply for every scheme you're eligible for. But we budget assuming none of them land — because they're announced after your deposit is due." |
| "It's too expensive." | "Compared to what? If it's compared to another quote, let's compare totals. If it's compared to what your family has, that's a real answer and I'd rather plan around it than pretend." |
| "Let me think about it." | "Of course. One thing that shouldn't wait: apply for your passport this week. What's the one thing that would make it a yes?" |
| "Can I pay everything at the end?" | "No — but you never pay for work we haven't done. It's four stages and each is invoiced when that stage is delivered. If you stop, the refund table applies." |
| "Will I get a job there?" | "I won't promise that. I'll show you the published work-rights rule and the date I checked it. What happens after depends on you." |
| "My parents aren't sure." | "Then let me talk to them directly. It's their money and it's not fair for them to hear the number from you second-hand." |
| "Can you get my fee reduced?" | "I can apply every published discount you qualify for and I'll show you the page. I can't negotiate a private rate, and an agency that says it can is usually taking it out of your service." |
| "What if I fail a subject?" | "Tell me now rather than in October. Most conditional offers survive it if we know early; almost none survive it if we find out at the deadline." |
| "Is Hungary any good?" | "For your budget and your profile, it's the option I can actually verify today. I'd rather sell you a country I've checked than one I've heard about." |
