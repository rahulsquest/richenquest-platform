# COUNTRY-INTELLIGENCE-MOAT.md — 2026-08-23

## Why RichenQuest is not a university search engine

A search engine answers **"does University X exist and what does it cost?"**

That question is already free. Collegedunia, LeapScholar, IDP and AECC all answer it, most of
them better resourced than RichenQuest will be for years, and several of them for free
because a lender or a university pays them instead.

**Competing there is unwinnable and unnecessary.**

The question that is *not* answered anywhere is:

> **"Given who I am and what my family has, what is the sequence of things that must happen —
> in what order, by when — for this to actually work?"**

That question has no search result. It has an answer per student, per country, per region,
per intake, and getting it wrong costs a family a year or the entire scholarship.

## The finding that proves the point

Italy's DSU grant is worth **€14,000–16,000 a year** and is open to non-EU students whose
family is resident abroad. For a Bihar family that is the difference between **₹30 lakh** and
**₹0–3.5 lakh** — the difference between impossible and possible.

To get it, a family must:

1. Obtain income and family-composition documents from Indian authorities
2. Have them **translated and legalised by the Italian Prefettura**
3. Submit them, **complete**, by a regional deadline that closes **before** the university's
4. Satisfy a regional ISEE process that differs by region — and may not even be required in
   one of them
5. Then keep the grant by meeting annual credit requirements

ER.GO's own bando states the documentation must be *"complete, translated and legalized"* by
the deadline, **"under penalty of being ineligible for benefits."**

**Total forfeiture, on a paperwork deadline, months in advance, in a language the family does
not read.** No amount of search solves that.

**That difficulty is the moat.** Not the list.

## What is actually defensible

| Layer | Copyable? |
|---|---|
| A list of universities | **Trivially.** It is public information |
| Cost figures | Easily — though most competitors don't source them |
| **Verified pathway intelligence** — which deadline binds, which document takes months, which region waives which step | **Hard.** It requires doing it repeatedly and recording what happened |
| **Outcome feedback** — what actually worked, per region, per profile | **Hardest.** It cannot be bought, only accumulated |

The first two are commodities. The last two compound, and only with real students.

## One honest correction to the positioning

The proposed line is *"AI-powered global mobility intelligence platform."*

**The matching engine contains no AI.** It is fully deterministic: five weighted dimensions,
every point traceable to a published field, reproducible on every run. That was a design
decision, not a limitation — a family is entitled to ask *why* an option was recommended, and
a deterministic rule can answer that in a sentence.

Calling it AI-powered would be the same class of overclaim the entire verification gate exists
to prevent. It would also be the first claim `qualityGate` would flag if a competitor's
brochure made it.

**A more accurate and, for this audience, stronger line:**

> **RichenQuest is a verified global education pathway platform. We tell you what a degree
> abroad will actually cost, which deadline really binds, and what to do next — and we show
> you where every figure came from.**

If a generative layer is added later, it explains and summarises; the score stays
deterministic, and that boundary is worth keeping.

## What each party gets

**Students** — one honest total instead of a tuition fee; the binding deadline named
correctly; a plan worked backwards from a real date; and an early "no" when it will not work,
while there is still a next intake.

**Parents** — the number the family must actually arrange, separated from the number that is
spent; sources for every figure; and reporting on **our work**, not surveillance of their
child.

**Universities and regional bodies** — applicants whose documents are complete and legalised
on time. For a DSU body, an incomplete file is administrative waste; RichenQuest reduces it.

**Partners** — teachers and community nodes get something checkable to hand a family, and get
told when the student they referred succeeded.

## The position this creates

**Before:** *"We are building an AI education platform."*

**Now:** *"We have built a verification-first pathway engine. Two universities are fully
verified, nineteen are honestly marked as not, and the engine refuses to recommend anything it
cannot source."*

The second is a weaker-sounding claim and a far stronger position, because **it is checkable**
— and the willingness to publish "2 of 21" instead of "300+ universities" is the single most
credible thing about the product.

## The one number that matters next
Not universities. Not features. **Students.** The intelligence layer compounds only against
real outcomes, and there are currently zero.
