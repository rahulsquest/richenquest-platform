# The RichenQuest Student Experience

**Every other document in this repository is written for the company. This one is written for the
student.**

---

## The design principle

**In this industry, trust is almost never destroyed by a bad answer. It is destroyed by silence.**

A family that has heard nothing for eleven days does not conclude *"they are busy."* They conclude
*"we have been forgotten,"* then *"we have been cheated."* And the periods of structural silence —
waiting for an offer, waiting for a visa, the first month abroad — are exactly the periods when the
family's fear is highest and the company has nothing new to say.

> **So the experience is designed around the silences, not the events.** Anyone can send a
> congratulations message. **The company that calls during the wait is the one they remember.**

---

## PHASE 1 · Suspicion → the first real number

**Days 0–7. The family has been lied to by three agencies already.**

| When | What happens | Who | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Within 2 hours** | *"We have your details. A counsellor will call you before [specific time]. Nothing to pay."* | auto | **The most important message in the entire journey.** It converts an anxious submission into a kept promise |
| **Within 48 hours** | The call. **Not a pitch.** Confirm the budget is total not annual, ask about the passport, ask who else decides | Counsellor | The three questions that reorder everything |
| **Within 5 days** | **The True Cost Report — with their name on it.** Every figure sourced and dated. What we have not verified, listed | Counsellor | **This is the unboxing moment** |
| Day 7 | *"Any questions on the report? Including the parts where we said we don't know."* | Counsellor | Invites challenge |

**The report is the artifact a father shows his brother.** It is the first time anyone has written down
what this will actually cost, addressed to their child by name. **Nothing else we produce gets
photographed and forwarded.**

**Trust risk here:** promising a callback time and missing it. **Never name a time we cannot keep.**

## PHASE 2 · Hope → fear, when the money becomes real

**Days 7–21. They have decided. Now the money is no longer hypothetical.**

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| At agreement | **Within 60 seconds of the first ₹15,000: a receipt, a named counsellor with a direct number, and a one-page "what happens next and when"** | auto |
| Same day | **The parent gets their own message** — not a copy of the student's. *"Your son has started. Here is what we will do, what it costs, and what we will not promise."* | Counsellor |
| Day 3 | Document checklist **with a date against every item**, passport first | Counsellor |
| Weekly | **A Friday update even when nothing moved** — *"No change this week. Your documents are with X. Next milestone is Y on [date]."* | Counsellor |

> **That Friday message is the cheapest trust in the business.** Most agencies go quiet when there is
> no news. **"Nothing changed this week" is news — it says somebody is still watching.**

**Trust risk:** taking money and going quiet. **The 60-second receipt exists because the gap between
paying and hearing anything is where doubt is born.**

## PHASE 3 · The application wait

**Weeks 3–8. Structurally silent. The most under-served period in the industry.**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| On submission | *"Submitted on [date] at [time]. Universities typically respond in X weeks. We will chase on [date]."* — **the chase date is stated in advance** |
| Every Friday | The update, even when empty |
| **On offer** | **Call the student. Then call the parent. Then — the 3× edge — message the teacher who introduced them** |
| **On rejection** | **Call within 24 hours. Never email a rejection.** Reasons sought from the university, alternatives within 72 hours |

**Trust risk:** the family discovering an outcome before we tell them. **They must always hear it
from us first.**

## PHASE 4 · Terror — the visa

**The highest-anxiety period of the entire journey.**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Before the appointment | **The 48-hour checklist**, and the sentence that prevents the worst surprise: *"From the day you submit, you cannot travel anywhere — not abroad, not domestically on your passport. Plan weddings and family events around this."* |
| Day of | **The counsellor is reachable by phone throughout the appointment window** |
| Same evening | *"Submitted. Tracking number X. Published processing time is Y — that is a range, not a promise. We will not guess a date."* |
| **Every 10 days** | **Check-in during the wait**, even with nothing to report |
| **On approval** | **Call the parent first.** They have carried this longer than anyone |
| **On refusal** | **Call within 24 hours. Grounds recorded verbatim.** *"Here is what it says. Here is what it means. Here is what we do next — and if this was our error, you are not paying that stage."* |

## PHASE 5 · Arrival — the loneliest week nobody designs for

**Days 1–30 in country. The industry stops here. This is where referrals are actually earned.**

| When | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Landing day | *"Message us when you reach your room."* | Someone is waiting to hear |
| **Day 1** | **The permit clock starts. Italy 8 days, Hungary 30, Malta 3 months.** Kit sent before departure, not after | Legal, not admin |
| **Day 3** | **A call. Not a form.** *"Have you eaten properly? Do you have a SIM? Is the room alright?"* | **The loneliest day of the journey — jet lag, wrong food, no bank, no phone.** Nobody in this industry calls on day 3 |
| Week 1 | The order that matters: permit → address → tax number → bank → SIM. **Wrong order wastes a week** |
| **Week 2** | **Ask for the review here** — gratitude decays. **Then ask for the introduction, not a sale** |
| **Week 3–4** | Parent update: *"He has his permit, his bank account is open, he has been to class."* | The parent has been imagining the worst for a fortnight |

## PHASE 6 · Months 2–4 — the dip

**Homesickness peaks around week 6. Grades wobble. Money runs short. Nobody is paid to notice.**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Week 6** | A call with no agenda. *"How is it actually going?"* |
| Month 3 | **First real cost check: what did it actually cost versus what we quoted?** → `Cost_Actual_INR` |
| Month 4 | Permit renewal timeline explained **before** it is urgent |

**This phase costs almost nothing and is where "I cannot imagine doing this with anyone else" is
formed.** It is also where the outcome data gets collected.

## PHASE 7 · The long relationship

Annual permit renewal reminder **60 days before expiry** · DSU credit-compliance check each semester
· festival wishes **from their counsellor by name, never a broadcast** · the graduation message · the
first-job congratulations · and the ask that closes the loop: **"Would you talk to a student from your
district who is where you were two years ago?"**

## Every moment trust can be lost — and the fix

| Moment | Fix |
|---|---|
| No reply after submitting | **2-hour auto-acknowledgement with a named callback time** |
| Callback promised, missed | **Never name a time we cannot keep** |
| Paid, then silence | **60-second receipt + counsellor's direct number** |
| Weeks with no news | **The Friday update, even when empty** |
| Learning bad news from elsewhere | **They hear everything from us first** |
| A number that turns out wrong | **Sourced and dated on the report; correction published** |
| Rejection by email | **Never. Call within 24 hours** |
| Passport surrendered without warning | **Said at submission, not when it bites** |
| Alone on day 3 | **The call** |
| Forgotten after arrival | **Week 2, week 6, month 3, every renewal** |
| Parent never contacted | **Their own messages, on our own work — never on the student's conduct** |
| A promise nobody could keep | **The five banned sentences** |

## What makes a student say *"I cannot imagine doing this with anyone else"*

**Not the admission. Not the visa. Not the price.**

Ask any family who has been through this and they remember two moments:

> ### 1. The day someone told them a true number that cost that person money.
> *"They told us Debrecen was ₹30 lakh, not ₹13. Everyone else had said thirteen."*
>
> ### 2. The day someone called during the silence.
> *"Nothing had happened. He called anyway, just to say nothing had happened."*

**Everything else in this document is the machinery that makes those two moments possible.**

**The first cannot be faked — it requires being paid by the family.**
**The second cannot be automated — it requires someone to actually call.**

**That is the whole product.**
