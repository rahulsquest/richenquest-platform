The Italy secret nobody explains properly

DSU grants: aid you receive by right, not by luck

Every Italian region runs a DSU (Diritto allo Studio Universitario — "right to study") program. Unlike competitive scholarships, DSU is need-based and awarded by right: if your family's documented income falls within the thresholds, you qualify.

For eligible international students it can include a full tuition-fee waiver, subsidised meals, housing support or dormitory priority, and an annual cash grant — often around €6,000, varying by region and housing status.

The catch: DSU paperwork (income documentation, deadlines, regional rules) defeats many applicants before they start. That is exactly the work our Italy-based team does — we prepare and file DSU applications on the ground, and we confirm your region's current figures during counseling.

Who typically qualifies

  • Families with modest documented income (thresholds set by each region)
  • Students at public universities across Italy
  • Renewable yearly if you keep passing your exams

Italy FAQs

What families ask us about Italy

Is studying in Italy really almost free?

For eligible families, close to it: income-based public fees can be a few hundred euros, and DSU can waive them entirely while adding a grant. Families above the income thresholds pay more — we tell you which case is yours before you commit.

Do I need to speak Italian?

Not for English-taught programs. Basic Italian helps daily life and part-time work, and universities offer courses — most of our students pick it up in their first year.

Can I work while studying in Italy?

Student permits allow part-time work up to about 20 hours a week. Treat it as a supplement for living costs, not a way to fund tuition — that's what DSU and honest budgeting are for.

What happens after graduation?

Italy offers a 12-month permit to stay and seek work, and your EU degree travels across Europe. Your counselor maps the realistic career paths for your field before you choose a program.
