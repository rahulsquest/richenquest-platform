## The gap between the brochure and the bill

We researched the University of Debrecen in Hungary carefully. It is a good university with a genuine
advantage for Indian students, and we recommend it to the right family.

**Its tuition for an English-taught Master's is USD 7,500–8,000 a year.** In rupees, about **₹13
lakh** across two years. That is the number most families are shown.

**The University of Debrecen's own cost-of-living page says students in Debrecen spend about USD 800
per month.** Over two years that is roughly **₹16.7 lakh** — more than the tuition.

| | |
|---|---|
| Tuition, 2 years | ₹13.05–13.92 lakh |
| Living, at the university's own published figure | **₹16.70 lakh** |
| Application fee + entrance exam, **non-refundable** | ₹0.43 lakh |
| Residence permit and entry visa | ₹0.10 lakh |
| **Total** | **₹30.29–31.16 lakh** |

**We still have not verified** health insurance, flights, document translation, or an emergency
buffer. **So even this is a floor, not a total.**

**The tuition figure was not wrong. It was just not the answer to the question a family is actually
asking.**

## Four things nobody mentions

**1. The application fee is not the application cost.**
Debrecen charges **USD 150 to apply**. There is also an entrance examination fee reported at **USD
350**. That is roughly **₹43,000 spent before anyone has looked at your file** — and it is not
refundable if the answer is no. *(We have asked the university to confirm the exam fee applies to
Master's applicants. Until they reply, treat it as unconfirmed.)*

**2. Where you live can change the total by ₹5–12 lakh.**
A university dormitory in Debrecen is reported at **HUF 40,000 a month**. Private rental, on the
university's own accommodation page, is **HUF 120,000–140,000**. Over two years that difference is
larger than most families' entire savings for the degree.
**We have asked whether dormitory places are guaranteed for self-funded international students. The
university's own page does not say they are.** Until we know, we plan on the higher number.

**3. Germany's €11,904 "blocked account" is not a cost.**
It is your own money. It sits in a German account and is **released back to you at about €992 a
month** to live on. Germany is a *liquidity* barrier — you must be able to produce the sum — not a
price. **Anyone describing it as a fee has not understood it.**

**4. Poland's rules on student work changed, and much of the advice online has not caught up.**
Since **1 December 2025**, a non-EU student in Poland can work without a separate work permit **only
if their university is on an approved Ministry of the Interior list** — and most Polish universities
are not. **If someone tells you your child can simply work part-time in Poland, ask them which list
the university is on.**

## The one that is genuinely worth knowing

**Italy's regional DSU grant is open to non-EU students, and it is means-tested on family income
rather than marks alone.**

The Emilia-Romagna authority's own rules state that an international student whose family does not
live in Italy is awarded at the **"fuori sede"** rate — **the highest band — regardless of where the
student actually lives.** For 2026/27 the national minimum for that band is **€7,171.11 a year**, and
DSU winners also have tuition waived and can receive accommodation and meal support.

**With DSU, an Italian public-university Master's can cost a family a fraction of the Hungarian
total.** Without it, Italy is still roughly ₹13–25 lakh.

**Three honest warnings, because this is the number families most want to believe:**

- **It is not guaranteed.** It is applied for, against income thresholds and available funding.
- **It arrives after enrolment.** You must be able to fund the course and the visa *before* it.
- **The applications close in early autumn** — typically July to September, **before most families have even chosen a university.** For a September 2027 start, that means preparing now.

**Anyone who tells you they can guarantee you this scholarship is lying to you.**

## The number nobody can give you

**How much must you show for a Hungarian student visa?**

Hungary's immigration authority requires you to prove you can cover subsistence, accommodation, return
travel and healthcare — **and publishes no figure at all.**

We found secondary sources confidently stating **HUF 200,000 a month** and others stating **HUF
98,000** — a difference of more than double. **They disagree because there is no published number to
agree on.**

> **If a consultant quotes you a precise amount for this, ask them where it comes from.**

## How to check us

Every figure above is from a primary source and dated. **Check them.**

- Debrecen tuition, deadlines, cost of living, accommodation — `edu.unideb.hu` and `unideb.hu`
- Hungarian residence permit fees and requirements — `oif.gov.hu`
- DSU rules and amounts — `er-go.it` and `edisu.piemonte.it`

**If you find we are wrong, tell us. We will correct it publicly and credit you.** We have already
corrected our own Debrecen estimate four times as we moved closer to primary sources — **and each time
the real number went up.**

**That is the whole reason this article exists.**
