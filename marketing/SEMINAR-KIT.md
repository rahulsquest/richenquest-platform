# Seminar kit — 45 minutes, students and parents together

> **Why this exists.** `outreach/REFERRAL-ENGINE.md` message B offers coaching institutes and schools
> *"a free 45-minute session for your students and their parents."* Until now nothing existed to
> deliver it. **That message was writing a cheque the company could not cash** — and the first
> principal who said yes would have exposed it.

**Every number in this deck traces to a verified university record or `claims.json`.** Where
something is unverified, the slide says so out loud — that is not a weakness in the talk, it is the
talk.

**Deliver in Hindi with English terms.** Parents are the decision-makers on money in this market and
they are the ones who will not ask a question in English.

---

## The one strategic choice in this deck

**It does not open with RichenQuest.** It opens with the four numbers that decide whether a family can
go abroad at all, and RichenQuest is not mentioned until minute 32.

> **A room in Patna has heard the "study abroad, unlock your future" pitch before, and discounted it.
> A talk that spends thirty minutes being useful and then says "we do this for a fee" converts far
> better than one that sells for forty-five.**

## SLIDES

### 1 · Title *(0–1)*
> **"Padhna hai bahar — par kitna kharcha aayega?"**
> *Studying in Europe: the real numbers*
> RichenQuest · Patna

**Say:** *"I'm going to spend the next 30 minutes on numbers, not motivation. If at the end you decide
Europe isn't for your family, this talk still did its job."*

### 2 · The one question *(1–3)*
> **Most families ask: "Which is the best university?"**
> **The question that decides everything is: "What is the total money required in Year 1?"**

**Say:** *"Rankings don't stop anyone from going abroad. Year-one cash does."*

### 3 · What people think it costs *(3–5)*
> **The assumption: ₹30–40 lakh**
> **That is roughly right — for the UK, Canada, Australia, and for private European institutions.**

**Say honestly:** *"If your target is the UK or Canada, that number is real. I'm not going to pretend otherwise."*

### 4 · Debrecen, Hungary — real numbers *(5–8)*
> **University of Debrecen** — verified 16 Aug 2026, from the university's own sources
> - Tuition: **USD 6,000–17,500/year.** Most programmes near **USD 6,500**
> - Master's: **USD 7,500–8,000/year**
> - **Non-refundable before any decision: USD 150 application + USD 350 entrance exam = USD 500**
> - Scholarships: merit **20% from Year 2** · alumni **10%** · sibling **10%**
> - ⚠️ **Living costs: we have not verified these for Hungary. We will not quote a number we haven't checked.**

**Say:** *"That last line is the most important slide in this deck. Sources disagree with each other by
double. I'd rather tell you I don't know than give you a number you budget on."*

### 5 · Vistula, Warsaw — real numbers *(8–10)*
> **Vistula University** — verified 16 Aug 2026
> - **Living cost ≈ €4,300/year**, derived from Poland's own statutory requirement — the figure the visa office itself uses
> - Offer decision in **2–3 days**
> - **Tuition: PENDING.** Their public pages don't publish it. **We've asked the university directly and we won't guess.**

**Say:** *"We have a rule: we don't say a price to a family unless it came from the university. We're waiting on this one."*

### 6 · So what does Year 1 actually look like? *(10–12)*
> **Hungary, typical programme:** tuition ≈ USD 6,500 + USD 500 non-refundable + living costs (**being verified**)
> **Poland:** tuition (**pending**) + living ≈ €4,300
>
> **Both are in a different universe from ₹30–40 lakh. Neither is free.**

### 7 · Door 1 — You may not need an IELTS certificate *(12–15)*
> **Debrecen:** requirement is **B2 = IELTS 5.5**, and a certificate is **not mandatory** if proficiency is shown **at interview**
> **Vistula:** you may apply **without a certificate** if your prior education was in English
>
> ⚠️ **Vistula's condition:** you must pass the university's own English test **on arrival**. **Fail it, and it's a preparatory course for at least one semester.**

**Say the condition out loud:** *"I could have left that second line off the slide and more of you would
be interested. A student who hears only 'no IELTS needed', reaches Warsaw, fails the test and loses a
semester — that's on whoever didn't tell them."*

### 8 · Door 2 — A study gap is not automatically fatal *(15–17)*
> **Debrecen accepts a study gap of up to 4 years**, and **India and Nepal are not on its restricted list**
> **Other universities differ. It's a per-university rule, not a general one.**

### 9 · Door 3 — The scholarship almost nobody applies for in time *(17–20)*
> **Italy's regional DSU grant is open to non-EU students** and can be worth **€14,000–16,000 a year** — tuition waiver, cash stipend, housing, meals
> It is **means-tested on family income**, not only on marks
>
> **Applications close in early autumn — before most families have even chosen a university.**

**Say:** *"This is the single most valuable thing in this talk. And the honest version is: if you want
it for 2027, we start now, and you go in September — not February. Anyone who tells you they'll rush
you into Italy in February is either not thinking or not telling you."*

### 10 · What goes wrong *(20–28)*
> **1. Applying too late.** Italy's visa can take **up to 90 days from submission** — official. A student who books an appointment 8 weeks before class has already lost the intake.
> **2. Paying non-refundable fees without being told.** USD 500 at one university before any decision.
> **3. Proof of funds in the wrong form.** Germany needs **~€11,904 deposited as cash**. Italy accepts **loans and scholarships**. Same student — one door shut, one open.
> **4. Missing the country's own portal step.** Universitaly, APS, e-Konsulat, AVATS. Get it wrong and the visa application is invalid regardless of how good the file is.
> **5. Believing a guarantee.** **Nobody can guarantee admission, a scholarship, or a visa.**

**Say:** *"Every one of these is preventable. None of them is about intelligence or marks."*

### 11 · The calendar *(28–32)*
> **February/March 2027 intake — Hungary, Poland.** Applying **now**
> **September 2027 intake — Italy, Germany.** Preparation starts **January 2027**; **Italy's scholarship window is July–September 2027**
>
> **The deadline that decides your intake is not the university's. It's the last safe date to file the visa.**

### 12 · What RichenQuest does *(32–36)*
> RichenQuest Private Limited · Patna, Bihar · incorporated January 2026 · team working in this field since 2024 · **1,000+ students guided**
>
> **We are paid by you, not by universities.** That is why we will tell you to wait seven months when waiting is worth €14,000 a year.
>
> **What we do:** verify every university against its own source and show you the date we checked · give you the total cost before you pay anything · plan backwards from the visa deadline · take you through the first month after you land.
>
> **What we charge: ₹60,000 — and you pay it in three parts.** ₹15,000 when we start. **₹30,000 only when you have an offer in your hand.** ₹15,000 when the visa is approved.

**Say:** *"You pay most of it when there is an offer in your hand. If we don't get you one, you haven't paid for one."*

### 13 · What we will not do *(36–38)*
> - We will **not** guarantee admission, scholarships or a visa
> - We will **not** quote a fee or a cost we have not verified
> - We will **not** claim a partnership we do not have
> - We will **not** tell you your "chances" as a percentage — **anyone who gives you a number is making it up**
> - We will **not** promise to get you a faster visa appointment. **Nobody legitimately can**

**Say:** *"This slide is the reason to choose us. Not the previous one."*

### 14 · Q&A and next step *(38–45)*
> **Free profile check.** Give us your marks, your budget band, your English status and any study gap. **We'll tell you which requirements you actually meet and which you don't — in writing, at no cost.**

**Capture:** paper slip or a QR to a form. Collect **name · phone · class/degree · budget band ·
English status · intended intake**. Those six fields are exactly what `leadToPlan` needs to produce a
full plan before the counsellor calls back.

## Parent-only variant (25 min)

Drop slides 7–8. Expand 6, 10 and 13. Add:

> **"Kya paisa wapas milega?"** — Government visa fees are generally **not** refundable. University
> application fees are **not** refundable. **Ask any consultant to tell you, in writing, what is and
> is not refundable — including their own fee.**

> **"Beta wahan akela kaise rahega?"** — Hungary requires converting to a residence permit **within
> 30 days of arrival**; Italy within **8 days**. **These are legal deadlines, not paperwork.** This is
> the part most agencies stop at, because they're paid when the student enrols.
